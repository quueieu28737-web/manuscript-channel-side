\subsection{External retrospective check: retain results, route measurements}\label{sec:utility-b}
We apply the procedure to a fixed external frame: both defenses in Deep Fingerprinting, all three in WFDefProxy, and all nine in Mathews et al.'s reevaluation, with DynaFlow's live implementation kept as a separate record \cite{sirinam2018deep,gong2021wfdefproxy,mathews2023critical}. The resulting 15 measurement records represent 14 defense--study pairs in three studies. The ledger retains favorable outcomes alongside weaknesses, fixes the Standard setting for the nine-defense table, and records a locator and a concrete measurement decision for every row (S35).

The main benefit is separating decisions that a mechanism name conflates (Table~\ref{tab:r28-external}). Stronger protected-data fitting requires revisiting WTF-PAD's attack result, while Walkie-Talkie's symmetric-pairing result remains a valid scoped outcome. FRONT's live protection improves even as its bandwidth cost changes. A record-level audit therefore preserves the security observation and replaces the cost estimate separately. DynaFlow's live result needs a separate execution/workload record, preventing a simulated cost from being attached to a live attack result.

The complete frame produces 11 protected-fit/view measurement requirements and four execution/cost requirements. Four favorable controls remain usable in their measured scopes: symmetric Walkie-Talkie, live FRONT, live Tamaraw and one-split TrafficSliver. The actionable output is a scoped retain, separate or remeasure decision for each record. These decisions agree with established WF practice. Outcomes were visible during extraction; the study is not a blinded failure-prediction test.

\subsection{Seven time-ordered internal judgments}\label{sec:utility-c}
The existing controlled study provides a complementary check: seven written judgments specify which apparent protection claims should survive a changed target, receiver or attacker. The protocol-only repository state predates the first archived result commit on 21 October 2026; S35 preserves the ordering receipt, original labels and current reassessment. Table~\ref{tab:r28-judgments} brings all seven into the main paper.

Four judgments have supporting counterexamples; one is partly supported. P5 cannot establish a transfer failure because protection at its source boundary was already absent. P7's proposed joint protection remains unestablished. These distinctions matter operationally: the audit calls for the right receiving view and protected-data fit, and rejects an unsupported source premise before attributing a failure to transfer. The decisions differ by failure mode: P1--P4 require the changed emitter, attacker or trace view to be measured; P5 requires establishing source protection first; P6/P7 require evidence for the still-open claim. This avoids spending a transfer study on a source condition that never established protection. Appendix~\ref{app:case-study} retains the complete measurement scopes and statistical qualifications.
