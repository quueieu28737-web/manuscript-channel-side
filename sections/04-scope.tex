\section{Result Records and Comparability Rules}\label{sec:scope}
The atomic unit of our systematization is one \emph{evaluated result record}, rather than a whole paper or a single headline score. A result record binds a source version and locator to seven structured fields: Source, Target (and threat polarity Types~I--IV), Receiver projection, Intervention boundary, Attacker training/calibration ($D_0$: clean-condition fit; $D_2$: protected-condition fit), Workload/holdout, and Outcomes (inference metric, task/component utility, and cost object; Table~\ref{tab:result-record}). A record is a comparison unit with a source-indexed parameter grid, not an independent trial. We split when a change of primitive, execution mode, target/receiver contract or separately measured cost object would alter what a result can support. We retain models, datasets, parameter values and repetitions as coordinates within that unit; numerical joins must preserve those coordinates. Source versions are reconciled within a family before extraction.

\paragraph{Indexing schema and comparison contracts}
The index names the hidden property, measured channel, observer permissions, intervention and evaluation provenance. Generating layers span hardware (P1), serving (P2), transport (P3), application/API (P4), orchestration (P5), tools/MCP (P6), environment (P7) and multi-agent communication (P8). We apply three claim-specific comparison contracts (Table~\ref{tab:comparison-contracts}). For the requested claim, a comparison is \emph{compatible} when its required fields align, \emph{incompatible} when a required object conflicts, and \emph{unresolved} when a required binding is unreported (U), unavailable (A) or unverified (?). In the audit, B means explicitly bound, P partial and -- inapplicable. A common inference topic permits a scoped description while costs or populations remain separate; comparing costs requires an aligned cost object. \paragraph{Record boundaries and coverage}
The 39-record ledger retains 31 privacy/attribution mitigation records from 19 families, two Type III evasion records and six contextual controls. The primary defense synthesis uses the first 31; the evasion records illustrate a different protected objective. A defense discussion without a scoped evaluated result stays in the map. Appendix~\ref{app:records} indexes the source scopes and split decisions. Three examples fix the granularity:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Split:} Early Bird's $K$-prefix recovery oracle and semantic-cache processing benchmark change both primitive and measured object; their numbers cannot form one protection--cost point.
\item \textbf{Retain coordinates:} SparLeak's model-by-replacement-intensity ASR/ROUGE grid remains one mitigation record. Each intensity's inference/quality pair must be retained together; selecting the lowest ASR and highest ROUGE from different rows is disallowed.
\item \textbf{Split outcome, share package:} CachePrune's direct recovery and contextual guessing require two target contracts. They reference one shared default quality/cost package, counted once when joining configurations.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Question cohorts precede compatibility judgments}
Records enter one declared comparison question by the intervention and receiving object, not by score or verdict: delivered traffic, service-cache reuse, local memory/resource behavior, behavioral humanization, generator fingerprinting or fixed instructions. Three contextual singletons concern access gates, contention and filter-created views. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction} lists all 39 assignments. When a mechanism spans several questions, retain the primary extracted question and document the alternative; a new cross-cohort question requires a new inventory. A shared cohort admits a pair for inspection; it does not make different targets or costs compatible. The archived pair index records a scoped match, a different-object witness or an unresolved binding for its stated comparison. A different-object label prohibits substituting that witness object, not every possible comparison between the papers. It is not an assessment of all three contracts. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction} varies record granularity and retains the original judgments. The reporting-pattern synthesis in Section~\ref{sec:defenses} uses item availability and within-record bindings directly.
