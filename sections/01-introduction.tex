\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM engines and agents expose packet sizes, cache timing, visited domains and tool sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. Defenses follow: padding, batching, cache isolation, decoy queries and action humanization. Each is evaluated at one receiver, execution boundary and workload, yet its headline number is cited as a property of the mechanism. A padding result measured on an upstream link says nothing about a frontend that re-emits decoded text; decoys whose read responses are discarded say nothing about cover that executes writes; and the overhead of a semantic cache does not price prefix isolation reported in the same paper.

Our central claim is practical: the framework turns dispersed defense evidence into \emph{traceable comparisons, configuration-bound outcomes and deployment measurement requirements}. A result record binds one outcome to its target, receiver projection, intervention boundary, attacker fit, workload and cost object (\S\ref{sec:scope}). The 82-family map locates these records in the field; the nine-item audit and six-axis holdout matrix establish their evidence scope. Together they answer one question: \emph{which defense claims does the literature support when a deployment changes?} Transfer boundaries here delimit the scope of an evaluated result and the evidence needed to extend it. External retrospective cases and internal judgments illustrate how readers can use these outputs.

\paragraph{Contributions}
The SoK contributes three connected results, each grounded in the complete literature audit:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Record-level comparison (\S\S\ref{sec:scope}--\ref{sec:defenses}).} The inventory screens 741 possible record pairs and adjudicates all 133 within-question pairs: 17 descriptive matches, 105 known mismatches and 11 unresolved bindings. Within-family contrasts expose substitutions of primitive, target and cost that paper-level summaries conceal. Cohort accounting and family-deletion checks make the dependence on grouping and record multiplicity inspectable.
\item \textbf{Configuration-level joint binding (\S\ref{sec:defenses}).} The 351 record--item cells identify which protection, utility and cost outcomes describe the same evaluated intervention. CachePrune's two records supply one inference--utility--cost package at a shared configuration. Deduplicating that package keeps record counts, family coverage and jointly supported operating points distinct.
\item \textbf{Deployment decisions (\S\S\ref{sec:evaluation}--\ref{sec:discussion}).} The 82-family, six-axis holdout map separates attack generalization from defended-condition evaluation and source-to-target defense transfer. Table~\ref{tab:r19-decisions} combines that map with comparison and joint-binding judgments to specify which evidence can be retained and which measurement a proposed extension requires. External retrospective cases and seven time-ordered internal judgments illustrate the use of these concrete outputs (\S\S\ref{sec:utility-b}--\ref{sec:utility-c}).
\end{itemize}
WF practice establishes adaptive attacks, distribution shifts and online-cost evaluation \cite{juarez2014critical,mathews2023critical}. The SoK extends this discipline to substitutions within a paper: prefix versus semantic-cache primitives, discarded reads versus executed state changes, and component quality versus workflow utility. Its positive reuse path retains a source-qualified candidate and its bound package, rather than only listing missing evidence.
