\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM engines and agents expose packet sizes, cache timing, visited domains and tool sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. Defenses follow: padding, batching, cache isolation, decoy queries and action humanization. Each is evaluated at one receiver, execution boundary and workload, yet its headline number is cited as a property of the mechanism. A padding result measured on an upstream link says nothing about a frontend that re-emits decoded text; decoys whose read responses are discarded say nothing about cover that executes writes; and the overhead of a semantic cache does not price prefix isolation reported in the same paper.

We therefore systematize defense evidence at the level of the \emph{evaluated result record} rather than the paper. A record binds one outcome to its target, receiver projection, intervention boundary, attacker fit, workload and cost object (\S\ref{sec:scope}). Three instruments operate on these records: a map of 82 study families by signal origin, receiving view and threat polarity; a nine-item audit of what each mitigation family binds; and a six-axis matrix of reported holdouts. Together they answer one question: \emph{which changes invalidate a measured defense result?}

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Record-level systematization (\S\S\ref{sec:methodology}--\ref{sec:discussion}).} Separating records exposes within-paper substitution: 8 of 15 same-family pairs in the complete 39-record audit measure different primitives, targets or cost objects, and only 17 of 133 related record pairs support even a descriptive comparison. Record-local joins identify a concrete reusable package: CachePrune alone binds inference, utility and cost, through two records sharing one configuration. The 2/20 record count describes this audit query, not independent replications or a field-wide prevalence. The 82-family holdout matrix identifies which transfer axes have source-bound evidence.
\item \textbf{Four boundary demonstrations (\S\ref{sec:controlled-placement}).} Controlled experiments using saved LLM responses and a retail agent workload instantiate the emitter, executed-state, calibration and attacker-observation boundaries identified by the audit, each priced with its own cost object.
\item \textbf{Calibration stability and deployment cost.} The GLM cancellation/return point contrast is $+20.8/-17.3$ BA points. We test all eight contrasts with customer-paired permutations and Holm correction, and rebuild calibration over 100 training-customer resamples to separate source changes from sampling and attacker-fit sensitivity. Table~\ref{tab:calibration-transfer} prices each target-specific contrast; Table~\ref{tab:r19-decisions} turns the findings into minimum re-evaluation requirements. Write-local shaping is inexpensive ($1.45$--$1.51\times$ bytes) but leaves return BA at 61.3--61.6\%, and a fresh live-planner study (37/50 versus 33/50 successes) bounds the utility claim of prefix cover.
\end{itemize}
WF practice already establishes adaptive attacks, distribution shifts and online costs \cite{juarez2014critical,mathews2023critical}. Our contribution is the record-level synthesis and the costed deployment decisions it enables (Table~\ref{tab:rq-findings}).
