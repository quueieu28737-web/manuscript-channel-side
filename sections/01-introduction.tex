\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM serving and agents expose packet sizes, cache timing, destinations and tool sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. Padding, cache isolation and decoy actions address different parts of this problem. A practitioner must decide which reported protection, quality and overhead measurements apply to the system they intend to deploy.

\paragraph{One example, three decisions}
Consider a serving operator choosing a cache-reuse defense. Early Bird evaluates a prefix-recovery oracle and separately measures semantic-cache deidentification overhead \cite[V-A--B]{earlybird2025}. CachePrune evaluates direct and contextual recovery and links its default configuration to generation quality and serving cost \cite[5.1--5.6]{wu2026cacheprune}. First, prefix and direct-recovery results can inform a shortlist for a shared token-recovery threat. Second, Early Bird's semantic-cache overhead cannot price the prefix policy, while CachePrune's shared package can inform feasibility at its tested configuration. Third, a changed workload or serving system needs the corresponding workload and cost measurements. The original papers need not have asked the operator's deployment question: the task is to reuse their results without changing what those results measure.

This SoK develops that task across LLM and agent side channels. We distinguish changes to the \emph{observed signal}, the \emph{executed intervention}, and the \emph{evaluation setting}. These categories connect low-level isolation and traffic shaping with a newer complication: a cover action can alter an agent's environment, and a component's output quality can differ from a planner's task success. The 82-family map supplies the landscape. The defense synthesis is deliberately bounded to 33 mitigation records from 20 families, with six contextual records used to explain observation and execution boundaries.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Comparison rules for scoped results.} We organize changes of signal, intervention and setting into explicit comparison questions (\S\S\ref{sec:scope}--\ref{sec:defenses}). The complete pair inventory includes source locators, reasons and sensitivity to record granularity. Its counts document the indexing choices; the useful output is the permission to retain a particular comparison.
\item \textbf{An atlas of outcomes measured together.} The record audit identifies which protection, utility and cost measurements share an evaluated configuration. Main-text identities and same-PDF query memberships let a reader reconstruct each supported combination and distinguish it from a union of family-level cells (\S\ref{sec:defenses}).
\item \textbf{Deployment measurement plans.} The holdout synthesis separates attack generalization from tested defense transfer. A reusable reporting card and filled examples translate a proposed deployment change into retained evidence and a specific next measurement (\S\S\ref{sec:evaluation}--\ref{sec:deployment}).
\end{itemize}
The synthesis builds on website-fingerprinting and adaptive-attack evaluation practice \cite{juarez2014critical,mathews2023critical,tramer2020adaptive,carlini2019evaluating}. Its added organizing question is which \emph{executed object and outcome} each measurement describes when serving primitives, tool state and planner utility coexist. External applications and internal examples show how to fill the evidence atlas and reporting card; they do not supply a comparative accuracy estimate for the procedure.
