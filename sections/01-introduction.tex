\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM engines and agents expose packet sizes, cache timing, visited domains and tool sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. Defenses follow: padding, batching, cache isolation, decoy queries and action humanization. Each is evaluated at one receiver, execution boundary and workload, yet its headline number is cited as a property of the mechanism. A padding result measured on an upstream link says nothing about a frontend that re-emits decoded text; decoys whose read responses are discarded say nothing about cover that executes writes; and the overhead of a semantic cache does not price prefix isolation reported in the same paper.

We therefore systematize defense evidence at the level of the \emph{evaluated result record} rather than the paper. A record binds one outcome to its target, receiver projection, intervention boundary, attacker fit, workload and cost object (\S\ref{sec:scope}). Three instruments operate on these records: a map of 82 study families by signal origin, receiving view and threat polarity; a nine-item audit of what each mitigation family binds; and a six-axis matrix of reported holdouts. Together they answer one question: \emph{which changes invalidate a measured defense result?}

\paragraph{A SoK of defense-evidence transfer}
Our central contribution is a record-level synthesis of when defense evidence can be reused. Controlled studies support this synthesis by measuring selected deployment changes. The family map locates the changes, the record audit establishes which outcomes can be joined, and the studies attach resource and behavioral consequences to the resulting decisions. This yields three connected contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identify the evidence that transfers (\S\S\ref{sec:methodology}--\ref{sec:evaluation}).} The complete 39-record audit finds object substitutions in 8 of 15 same-family pairs; 17 of 133 related pairs support a source-qualified descriptive comparison. Configuration-linked joins identify CachePrune's single inference--utility--cost package through two records. The 82-family, six-axis holdout map distinguishes reported input separation from evidence for a changed generator or environment.
\item \textbf{Measure the consequences of selected boundary changes (\S\ref{sec:controlled-placement}).} Four controlled studies connect emitter placement, stateful execution, calibration and attacker observation to scoped inference, utility and resource costs. Emitter/state studies reproduce mechanisms and measure their operating consequences; calibration and timing studies examine local training variability, residual operation signatures and live execution conditions.
\item \textbf{Turn the synthesis into deployment decisions (Table~\ref{tab:r19-decisions}).} Each changed boundary has a minimum re-evaluation requirement. For calibration, the 100-resample study establishes the local baseline needed before attributing a contrast to its source model. For execution, a fresh 100-episode planner study measures prefix-cover success under response deadlines. These measurements support decisions at their own operating points.
\end{itemize}
