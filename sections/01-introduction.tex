\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM serving and agents expose packet sizes, cache timing, destinations and tool sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. Padding, cache isolation and decoy actions address different parts of this problem. Choosing among them requires both a protection result and evidence of what the protected system still does, at what cost.

\paragraph{What the literature reports together}
The inspected mitigation literature emphasizes inference outcomes more consistently than utility or matched adaptive evaluation. Explicit bindings occur in 16/17 applicable families for inference, 4/17 for utility and 2/11 for matched adaptation. This contrast survives accepting partial evidence: 17/17, 6/17 and 2/11, respectively. Cost is explicitly available in 11/19 families, yet five of the eight families with both inference and cost place the two in different records. These are observable patterns of reporting in the extracted sources. They motivate the SoK's central question: \emph{which evidence describes the same protected system, and what additional measurement does a deployment change require?}

\paragraph{One example, three decisions}
Consider a serving operator choosing a cache-reuse defense. Early Bird evaluates a prefix-recovery oracle and separately measures semantic-cache deidentification overhead \cite[V-A--B]{earlybird2025}. CachePrune evaluates direct and contextual recovery and links its default configuration to generation quality and serving cost \cite[5.1--5.6]{wu2026cacheprune}. Prefix and direct-recovery results inform a shortlist for a shared token-recovery threat. Early Bird's semantic-cache overhead prices a different mechanism, whereas CachePrune's shared package informs feasibility at its tested configuration. A changed workload then identifies the next workload and cost measurements. This example connects the reporting pattern to a concrete selection decision.

The 82-family map supplies context. The defense synthesis covers 33 mitigation records from 20 families, with six contextual records locating observation and execution boundaries. We organize changes into the \emph{observed signal}, \emph{executed intervention} and \emph{evaluation setting}. This taxonomy connects familiar traffic-shaping and isolation questions with agent-specific issues: a cover action can change the environment, and component output quality can differ from task success.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A synthesis of reporting asymmetries.} Source-linked family and record censuses identify where inference, utility, adaptation and cost evidence are available, including the agent task-utility gap. Status relaxation, common denominators and family-removal checks distinguish stable patterns from strict-binding-dependent findings (\S\ref{sec:defenses}).
\item \textbf{Configuration-level comparison and positive reuse.} The record atlas separates costs measured for another mechanism and retains outcomes measured together, including the CachePrune package and SparLeak's intensity-specific inference/quality pairs. Exact record identities make each combination reconstructable in this PDF (\S\ref{sec:defenses}).
\item \textbf{Deployment measurement plans.} The holdout synthesis separates attack generalization from tested defense transfer. A reusable reporting card and filled examples translate a changed signal, intervention or setting into retained evidence and a specific next measurement (\S\S\ref{sec:evaluation}--\ref{sec:deployment}).
\end{itemize}
The synthesis builds on website-fingerprinting and adaptive-attack evaluation practice \cite{juarez2014critical,mathews2023critical,tramer2020adaptive,carlini2019evaluating}. It connects that practice to the measured objects in LLM/agent systems, where serving primitives, tool state and planner utility coexist. Worked applications illustrate the resulting decisions.
