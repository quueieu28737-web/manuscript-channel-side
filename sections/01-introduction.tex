\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals content while serving caches, packet streams and tool executions expose other observations \cite{earlybird2025,netecho2025,jeong2026wra}. A deployment can keep the name of a defense while changing what an observer sees, which state its cover affects, or how the attacker is trained. Choosing a mechanism is therefore only the start of a deployment decision. The next question is: \emph{which protection, function and cost evidence can be retained across the proposed change?}

\paragraph{One defense name can refer to different evidence}
CachePrune's selective-sharing results can inform a configuration-specific feasibility assessment \cite[5.1--5.6]{wu2026cacheprune}. Early Bird's prefix policy and semantic-cache prototype require separate cost decisions \cite[V-A--B]{earlybird2025}. These distinctions follow from the observer, primitive, operating point and measurement object. A shared mechanism or dataset name does not establish paired observations. The useful outcome is a scoped candidate and a measurement decision.

\paragraph{A proposed framework follows three boundaries}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Receiving view.} A protected representation can be followed by another emitter or observable. Retain the exact receiver and permitted fields, not a blanket privacy label.
\item \textbf{Affected state.} Discarding a reply and preventing an action's effects are different operations. Retain a content-preservation witness where it applies; separately establish task outcomes for added execution.
\item \textbf{Attacker and workload.} A clean-trained classifier, a defense-aware attacker and a source-calibrated defense establish different objects. Retain their fitting conditions and holdout units with their measured function and cost.
\end{itemize}
This is a methodological position paper, not a comprehensive literature review. The framework and reporting card are proposals: neither analyst benefit nor coding reliability has been established. Section~\ref{sec:agenda} describes a future blinded coding and analyst-comparison study; no such study was conducted here.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item We organize evidence reuse around three boundaries: receiving view, affected execution state, and attacker and workload.
\item We propose a source-linked reporting card (Table~\ref{tab:r33-card}) that records the protection, function, cost and measurement links needed for a reuse decision.
\item We apply these distinctions to two bounded cases: E4 is a finite synthetic TLS replay study of attacker fitting and calibration; R44 is a retail-sandbox study of extra tool actions and task utility. They illustrate the reporting choices, rather than validate the card.
\end{itemize}
The case material is selected purposefully. Its provenance, nested counts and outstanding identity and eligibility questions are retained in the supplement. Existing website-fingerprinting and adversarial-evaluation guidance supplies essential precedents; Section~\ref{sec:corpus-map} compares the proposed card with those traditions.
