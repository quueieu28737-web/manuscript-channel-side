\paragraph{Whole-ledger synthesis}
Each record has one primary measurement mode: 20 executed collections/prototypes, six trace transformations, five simulations/derived estimates, six component/calibration measurements and two unresolved placements (Table~\ref{tab:r9-record-index}). Eleven of the 20 mitigation families contribute more than one record, and nine of them span more than one mode; this is why a mechanism name cannot carry utility or cost between records. Executed collection establishes its stated execution scope, not automatically full-workflow utility.

\subsection{Comparison permissions and their sensitivity to granularity}
The declared question groups generate 133 comparisons from 741 possible record pairs; 608 have no proposed comparison. Table~\ref{tab:r33-pairs} reports every within-question decision. A descriptive match permits a source-qualified shortlist or discussion of the same target, while retaining different costs and populations; it does not permit effect pooling. For example, Early Bird's prefix oracle and CachePrune's direct recovery can both inform a token-recovery shortlist. They do not share a measured serving-cost population.

\paragraph{What the mismatch count means}
The 105 different-object pairs and eight of 15 same-family pairs are conditional outputs of the split and grouping rules. Some differences are the reason the records were split. We accordingly make no claim that these fractions reveal an empirical failure rate. Distinct targets, hardware, operating points and research objectives are normal design choices. The avoidable mistake belongs to a \emph{reuse operation}: attaching Early Bird's semantic-cache cost to its prefix oracle, or attaching an offline timing transform's cost to a live scheduler. The inventory does not establish that the source authors made that mistake, and does not require every defense study to be mutually comparable.

\paragraph{What changes when records are merged}
Table~\ref{tab:r33-granularity} reprojects the fixed decisions using three coarser units. A projected pair is unanimous only if all constituent judgments agree; otherwise it remains mixed. Family-only grouping hides all 15 within-family pairs and yields 44 cross-family pairs, eight mixed. Merging the two CachePrune targets at their shared package yields 38 units and 124 pairs. These are indexing sensitivities, not newly adjudicated studies; a coarser unit can conceal a difference without resolving it. The original 17/133 is therefore not assigned a ``high/low'' interpretation against a random baseline. Randomly permuting question membership would mostly create unjudged cross-question pairs and cannot provide a null distribution for validity.

\paragraph{Three useful outputs}
A descriptive match retains scoped alternatives. A documented object change requires a measurement at the proposed new object. An unresolved link requires source clarification. This division tells a practitioner what to do next without treating a normal difference of research scope as a defect. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction} and the machine-readable pair inventory expose membership and reasons; family-deletion counts in S33 are dependency diagnostics only.

\paragraph{Join evidence at the record, not the family}
A family-level B means that at least one record supplies an item. It does not imply that its other B cells concern the same intervention. We therefore code all 351 record--item cells and join only B bindings within the same record and configuration (Table~\ref{tab:r19-joint}). All joint queries begin with 33 mitigation records. A query excludes a record only when a required item is inapplicable; U, ?, and P remain eligible but unbound. Table~\ref{tab:r19-joint} separates excluded, eligible and bound counts, with deduplicated families/configurations. The six contextual controls are separate. CachePrune supplies the triple join: two outcomes share one quality/cost package. The denominator identifies eligible records for our query, not papers obliged to answer it. \paragraph{Reading the family and record tables together}
Eight family rows have B in both inference and cost, but only three families have those bindings in the same record. Table~\ref{tab:r33-positive} lists all five positive records; Table~\ref{tab:r33-crosswalk} shows the eight family projections and their exact supporting records in the same PDF. Early Bird, for example, supplies inference in its prefix-oracle row and cost in its semantic-cache row. CachePrune's two targets instead point to one default package. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction} gives all 33 records' membership in all five queries, making the differing denominators visible.

These inventories answer specified feasibility questions. The sole triple package is a useful worked configuration, not a robust estimate of how often the field reports complete evidence. Removing CachePrune removes that example. A published-version-linked subset does not supply another triple package in the inspected records (S36); its small eligible denominators do not support a venue-quality comparison. A new source report can extend the atlas without changing the comparison rules.
