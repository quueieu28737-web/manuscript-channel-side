\paragraph{Sensitivity distinguishes two adaptation questions}
Accepting P raises inference to 16/16, utility to 5/16 and cost to 15/18, while matched adaptation remains 2/10. \subsection{Agent privacy evidence lacks an explicit task-utility binding}
We select agent privacy/attribution families by P5 generation and Type I or II in the core map. All four members are visible: UI Traces (R8 U), WRA (P), Dangol (U), and the cloud traffic benchmark (P). WRA reports separately judged usefulness; the cloud benchmark asserts semantic preservation. Neither provides the same-condition task score required by R8 B. Hence the subset has 0/4 explicit and 2/4 explicit-or-partial utility bindings. This pattern survives both removal checks. It is a gap in \emph{explicit task-utility binding}, not absence of all functional evidence.

The three Type I/II R8 B families show what existing utility evidence can support. TDXRay binds vocabulary coverage to lookup behavior; CachePrune binds generation quality to its serving configuration; SparLeak pairs recovery and ROUGE by intensity \cite{hornetz2026tdxray,wu2026cacheprune,chen2026sparleak}. AHB binds task accuracy for Type III evasion and is retained only as a boundary example \cite{zhu2026turing}. This distinction identifies the task metric needed when a privacy cover method is moved into an acting planner.

\subsection{Family-level inference and cost often describe different records}
Eight families have explicit inference and explicit cost somewhere in their evaluations. In five---Early Bird, SpliceLeak, Time Will Tell, KVGov and OTRO---no individual record carries both. The distinctions are concrete: prefix policy versus semantic processing; background-load mitigation versus QCP; a length instruction versus a padding estimate; partial flushing versus HMAC; and padded versus unpadded ORAM. SPEC, Dangol and CachePrune supply the five same-record inference/cost results listed in Table~\ref{tab:r35-positive}. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction} lists all eight families' exact supporting records.

This separation remains five of seven families after removing CachePrune and four of six after removing the final-search additions. Accepting partial cost and inference evidence reduces it to three of 12. Record splitting by mechanism or cost object contributes to the strict count, so these counts are an inventory of the chosen evidence units, not an independent rate of research shortcomings. The underlying source examples, rather than the ratio, determine whether a cost can price a proposed mechanism.

The original R4/R5 ask specifically about fitting on protected data and a matched clean/protected fit. Their inapplicable status for a non-learning attacker does not answer whether another defense-aware attack is possible. A broader adaptive-attack question needs fresh source coding; treating those cells as unknown changes the denominators without inventing evidence (Table~\ref{tab:r36-sensitivity}). Requiring explicit execution placement also removes SPEC's payload-cost record from the inference--cost query. The five retained cost objects in the original query are not interchangeable.

In a leave-one-family-out check, the two triple-binding records disappear only when CachePrune is removed; removing any of the other 18 families leaves both records. Removing all final-search additions leaves no triple-binding record among 15 eligible records. These are sensitivity descriptions for this corpus, not estimates of field-wide prevalence.

\subsection{Execution evidence is distributed across measurement modes}
Among the 31 privacy/attribution records, 13 are executed collections/prototypes, five trace transformations, five simulations/derived estimates, six component/calibration measurements and two unresolved placements. The 12-record network-receiver subset contains six executed, four transformed and two unresolved records. These modes are a mixture rather than a predominance of network replay. An executed prototype still supplies only its measured local scope.

\paragraph{Comparison permissions as a supporting index}
The declared question groups generate 133 indexed pairs; 17 retain a scoped match for the object examined. This label does not by itself align populations, costs or paired outcomes. Early Bird's prefix oracle and CachePrune's direct-recovery result can inform the same token-recovery shortlist while keeping their costs separate. Record splitting and question grouping determine this inventory; it guides a specific comparison rather than measuring a field-wide defect rate. Complete decisions and coarser-granularity projections are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction}. The reporting findings above are recomputed from cells and records, independently of pair counts.

\soktakeaway{SoK Takeaway 2 (Bind function and cost to protection)}{Inference reporting is widespread in the inspected mitigation sources; explicit utility and matched protected-data fitting contrasts are much less common. Check the measured operating point before combining protection and cost, and request a task outcome when a privacy intervention changes agent execution.}
