\paragraph{Sensitivity distinguishes two adaptation questions}
Accepting P raises inference to 17/17, utility to 6/17 and cost to 15/19, while matched adaptation remains 2/11. Protected fitting rises sharply from 4/11 to 9/11. Thus fitting is often partially described; the larger gap is the explicit link to the protected condition and the matched adaptation contrast. In the common 14-family population, accepting P gives 14 inference, five utility and ten cost bindings. Expanding adaptation applicability to all 20 families, treating previously inapplicable entries as U only for this stress check, gives 4/20 protected fitting and 2/20 matched adaptation, or 9/20 and 2/20 with P. Removing CachePrune or all six final-search records preserves the inference--utility contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction}).

\subsection{Agent privacy evidence lacks an explicit task-utility binding}
We select agent privacy/attribution families by P5 generation and Type I or II in the core map. All four members are visible: UI Traces (R8 U), WRA (P), Dangol (U), and the cloud traffic benchmark (P). WRA reports separately judged usefulness; the cloud benchmark asserts semantic preservation. Neither provides the same-condition task score required by R8 B. Hence the subset has 0/4 explicit and 2/4 explicit-or-partial utility bindings. This pattern survives both removal checks. It is a gap in \emph{explicit task-utility binding}, not absence of all functional evidence.

The four R8 B families show what existing utility evidence can support. TDXRay binds vocabulary coverage to lookup behavior; CachePrune binds generation quality to its serving configuration; SparLeak pairs recovery and ROUGE by intensity \cite{hornetz2026tdxray,wu2026cacheprune,chen2026sparleak}. AHB binds executed task accuracy to its wrapper, but its goal is detector evasion, not the privacy/attribution objective of the four-family subset \cite{zhu2026turing}. This distinction identifies the task metric needed when a privacy cover method is moved into an acting planner.

\subsection{Family-level inference and cost often describe different records}
Eight families have explicit inference and explicit cost somewhere in their evaluations. In five---Early Bird, SpliceLeak, Time Will Tell, KVGov and OTRO---no individual record carries both. The distinctions are concrete: prefix policy versus semantic processing; background-load mitigation versus QCP; a length instruction versus a padding estimate; partial flushing versus HMAC; and padded versus unpadded ORAM. SPEC, Dangol and CachePrune supply the five same-record inference/cost results listed in Table~\ref{tab:r33-positive}. Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction} lists all eight families' exact supporting records.

This separation remains five of seven families after removing CachePrune and four of six after removing the final-search additions. Accepting partial cost and inference evidence reduces it to three of 12; a partial cost estimate can reconnect an operating point without supplying the fully bound cost. The strict majority is therefore specific to \emph{explicit} bindings. It does not justify calling all remaining cost evidence a microbenchmark or an end-to-end measurement.

\subsection{Execution evidence is distributed across measurement modes}
Among the 33 mitigation records, 14 are executed collections/prototypes, six trace transformations, five simulations/derived estimates, six component/calibration measurements and two unresolved placements. The frequently cited 20/39 executed total includes all six contextual controls. Restricting instead to network receivers (P3 observation) yields 12 mitigation records: six executed, four transformed and two with unresolved placement. The data therefore support a mixture of execution and transformation, not a predominance of network replay. Excluding final-search records leaves 11/27 executed mitigation records. Even an executed prototype supplies only its measured local scope; the six component/calibration records include a hardware timing baseline as well as component-cost measurements.

\paragraph{Comparison permissions as a supporting index}
The declared question groups generate 133 comparisons, of which 17 retain a descriptive comparison. Early Bird's prefix oracle and CachePrune's direct-recovery result can inform the same token-recovery shortlist while keeping their costs separate. Record splitting and question grouping determine this inventory; it guides a specific comparison rather than measuring a field-wide defect rate. Complete decisions and coarser-granularity projections are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction}. The reporting findings above are recomputed from cells and records, independently of pair counts.

\soktakeaway{SoK Takeaway 2 (Bind function and cost to protection)}{Inference reporting is widespread in the inspected mitigation sources; explicit utility and matched adaptation are much less common. Check the measured operating point before combining protection and cost, and request a task outcome when a privacy intervention changes agent execution.}
