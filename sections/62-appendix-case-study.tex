\section{Illustrative Case Study and Reconstruction Scope}\label{app:case-study}\label{app:equivalence}
This appendix explains the role of the archived implementation examples. They instantiate the same distinctions used in the literature atlas: final receiver versus upstream trace, executed state versus discarded responses, and online planner utility versus reused trajectories. They are illustrative applications, not validation data for the comparison rules. The complete numerical grids, negative outcomes, intervals and corrected tests remain unchanged in S11--S32.

The transport example has 48 saved responses on 24 topics (12 train/12 test). The public \emph{retail} setting has 115 tasks from 53 customers; its frozen split contains 46 test tasks from 24 customers, including 5 cancellation-positive and 7 return-positive customers. The separate live planner example has 100 episodes: 37/50 baseline and 33/50 defended successes, a difference of $-8.0$ points with customer interval $[-23.9,6.5]$. All eight calibration contrasts have Holm-adjusted $p=1.000$; the original absolute protection screen remains unmet. Neither these unresolved contrasts nor resampling the same customers validates the framework.

The artifact separates \emph{analysis reconstruction} from \emph{new model collection}. Archived requests, responses, traces, calibration hashes and predictions permit the reported calculations to be reconstructed. Fresh calls to mutable Sol/Luna/MiniMax/GLM aliases, with documented unequal budgets, cannot reproduce an identical generator. No fixed-weight replication claim is made. Historical protocols and model identities remain explicit in S14--S32; the literature synthesis does not depend on obtaining a favorable result from these examples.
