The record atlas and source cards organize evidence requests for a proposed reuse. \subsection{Case 1: bind the final target-bearing emitter}
NetEcho's upstream packet-size shaping operates on a different projection from the tool-call sequence. Its plaintext-aware frontend then emits a new size trace (grouped token counts or character lengths), reopening a size channel after upstream protection \cite{netecho2025}. SplitDecoy is a separate map-only return-path example, outside the 39-record mitigation ledger: the cloud sees nonzero gradient row support on every tested frame across nine seeds \cite[Sections 5.1--5.3]{politis2026splitdecoy}. Both change the receiver boundary and therefore require a separately bound inference result.

SICC further isolates length/structure-based inference after message content is authorized \cite[Table~3]{xu2026sicc}. DP-MemView's separate diagnostic exposes an internal score while holding compliant view-selection and response paths fixed, yielding an attribute AUC of 0.976 \cite[RQ2, following Table~4]{dpmemview2026}. This is a different released view, not an attack on the compliant interface. The evidence card therefore retains the semantic privacy result and separately rejects applying it to the exposed score.

\subsection{Case 2: bind utility to the action that executes}
WRA discards decoy reads for Type~I confidentiality; AHB's Type~III evasion executes GUI events, a different objective and state contract \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing}. A preserved response and an unchanged database therefore establish different functional claims. Our fresh retail-sandbox study tests this distinction with separately executed planner/user interactions and cloned initial states; these share tasks and services and are not statistically independent trials. After the first eligible order lookup, the three arms add nothing, one hidden read, or one hidden same-item write. The write's response is discarded, but its payment-ledger and modification-quota effects remain visible to subsequent execution.

In the mutable Qwen service-alias cohort, 187 three-arm groups cover 98 tasks selected nonrandomly from incomplete GLM blocks. The selected subset retains the protocol's randomized execution order and arm permutations; this does not make the cohort representative. The 113 complete GLM groups remain a separate exploratory description. Averaging each task's available repeats before giving tasks equal weight, write-cover success is 46.9 percentage points below baseline (95\% task-bootstrap interval $[-56.8,-36.9]$). The read-cover difference is $+0.7$ points with interval $[-4.9,6.1]$, leaving small gains or losses unresolved. This is a deliberately state-changing utility stress condition, not a measurement of WRA's privacy protection. All 97 executed cover writes consume the sandbox's modification eligibility; payment-ledger and quota semantics make the direction of harm unsurprising. The study does not isolate how much of the difference each mechanism causes, test an online side-effect-free write control or estimate a general cost of write cover. Earlier reference-program shadow checks do not provide that missing fresh-planner control. Recovery changed concurrency from 10 to 2 and the transport path; ten interrupted request waits and server outcomes remain unknown. These limits accompany the numerical difference. Appendix~\ref{app:boundary-experiments} retains all three arms, the paired tests and the cohort boundary.

\subsection{Case 3: bind adaptation, calibration and cost to the tested setting}
The replay study uses three pinned local model artifacts, 100 synthetic topics with five templates each, and a 128-token generation budget. The binary task distinguishes 50 medical from 50 nonmedical topics, with five topic-held-out folds and a 50\% chance balanced accuracy (BA). Saved output chunks are emitted through timed TLS loopback. This finite setting does not establish outcomes for cloud services, real-user topics, WANs or defenses operating during generation.

For each defended test view, $D_0$ fits on clean training data and $D_2$ on defended training data. At Qwen~8B's default four-chunk batching point, the matched three-learner raw reference is 79.0\% BA, versus 50.0\% for $D_0$ and 73.2\% for $D_2$. Each condition separately selects its best logistic regression, random forest or gradient boosting result on the same five-fold out-of-fold predictions used for reporting. These are exploratory condition-wise suite maxima, not a paired effect estimate for one fixed classifier. Selection can make them optimistic; neither nested selection nor a separate evaluation set is used. Moreover, $D_0$ logistic regression has AUROC 0.678 despite BA 50.0\%: the thresholded result does not imply absence of discriminative information.

The calibration audit also limits the claim. Default bucket, batch and fixed-cell parameters are identical across all source folds, so their zero imported-minus-local differences are degenerate comparisons with no evidence about transferring a changed calibration. Some hard-cap source/fold comparisons have different parameters, while others remain identical. At the reported precision, five of the six conditional intervals include or touch zero; only Llama~1B to Qwen~8B excludes zero, at $+3.4$ attack-BA points with interval $[0.8,5.4]$. Higher attack BA means worse protection for that imported cap under this comparison, not improved defense utility. The secondary Holm adjustment covers two imports per target, not all targets, policies and points. Appendix~\ref{app:boundary-experiments} reports all six and the omitted fitting and selection uncertainty. The evidence neither establishes a robust transfer effect nor demonstrates equivalence or absence of a source effect. No reported local fitting-variability control separates a source change from finite-sample calibration variation.

Cost must follow the same configuration. For those 500 Qwen~8B batching replays, response application TLS bytes total 470,239 versus 1,234,823 for paired raw captures (0.381$\times$), because aggregation reduces framing and record overhead. Paired completion differences have median $+0.152$~ms and 95th percentile $+2.381$~ms; the 95th-percentile delay of original chunk endpoints is $+65.730$~ms. These are local replay measurements over 100 topics, excluding requests and handshakes. They do not price online generation or a WAN. The next deployment measurement is an adapted attacker and a nondegenerate, locally controlled calibration comparison, with inference and cost bound to that same execution. The appendix and supplement retain the incomplete original run and separate recovery-arm provenance.

\subsection{Bounded reappraisal of published results}
The existing external ledger covers 15 measurement records for 14 defense--study pairs in Deep Fingerprinting, WFDefProxy and Mathews et al.; these were retrospective applications with the published outcomes visible, not new experiments or independent predictions. Two examples make the assessment inspectable. WFDefProxy's FRONT comparison reports DF TPR 72.06\% versus 42.79\%, FPR 2.16\% versus 2.49\%, and data overhead 52\% versus 75\% \cite[6.2, Table~10]{gong2021wfdefproxy}. The retained assessment keeps each observed protection/cost tuple; it does not attach simulated cost to a live outcome or rank protection at a common FPR. Mathews et al.'s DynaFlow live result reports 379\% mean per-site time overhead, but changed URLs and crawler prevent treating its simulation/live difference as a matched causal effect \cite[IV-F, Table~II; Appendix~B]{mathews2023critical}.

These distinctions were already supported by the cited papers. The LLM application makes the analogous join explicit: Early Bird's approximately 6\,ms semantic-cache de-identification cost does not price its prefix rule, while CachePrune's default-configuration association still does not establish paired protection/serving trials (\S\ref{sec:cache-decisions}). The concrete output is a source-located acceptance or rejection of that particular join, not discovery of a new source-paper error or evidence that analysts using the card outperform existing practice. The full external ledger, including favorable scoped results, remains in the supplement.

\paragraph{What the three findings add}
The contribution is an auditable organization of source-specific comparisons: which observation a result covers, which state a tool action changes, and which fitting or calibration quantities differ. The source examples show what a reporting card records; E4 and R44 supply bounded measurements of selected cases. They do not establish new general evaluation principles, reliable analyst judgments or improved deployment outcomes. Those claims remain untested.

\subsection{A deployment query determines the evidence to retain}
Start with the intended receiver, protected outcome and intervention (Figure~\ref{fig:main-decision}). Record-level comparison identifies whether the cited result measures that object. Configuration-level joint binding identifies which inference, utility and cost outcomes share its configuration. Deployment decisions use the holdout matrix to identify evidence for the proposed change and specify the remaining measurement. Table~\ref{tab:r19-decisions} proposes the following evidence requests: a new emitter needs a receiver-specific capture, writes need state/authorization checks, source-calibration comparisons need a local fitting-variability control, and an online scheduler needs its own planner utility and latency measurement. The calibration control follows from the comparison design: changing the source can also change the finite training sample used to fit the defense.
