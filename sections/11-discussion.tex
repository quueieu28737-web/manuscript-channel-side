\section{Three Synthesis Lenses over the Result Ledger}\label{sec:discussion}
We select examples retrospectively by the \emph{comparison decision}, not the size of a reported effect: a changed receiving path, cover with different state effects, or an adaptation/cost mismatch within a named mechanism. NetEcho/SplitDecoy, WRA/AHB and the component-cost records instantiate these three lenses. They are explanatory examples, not a representative sample. The census in \S\ref{sec:defenses} covers all 39 records; the RQ3 matrix covers all 82 families, including those outside the mitigation ledger.

\subsection{Case 1: bind the final target-bearing emitter}
\paragraph{The downstream re-emission boundary}
NetEcho's upstream packet-size shaping operates on a different projection from the tool-call sequence. Its plaintext-aware frontend then emits a new size trace (grouped token counts or character lengths), reopening a size channel after upstream protection \cite{netecho2025}. SplitDecoy is a separate return-path record: the cloud sees nonzero gradient row support on every tested frame across nine seeds \cite[Sections 5.1--5.3]{politis2026splitdecoy}. The inference is a changed receiver boundary, requiring a separately bound inference result.

The data processing inequality requires $S\to Z\to Y$: target-oblivious postprocessing cannot increase $I(S;Z)$ \cite[Theorem~2.8.1]{cover2006elements}. Plaintext-aware re-emission or backward computation changes that premise. The synthesis locates this premise change in published records; Exp~1 measures its streaming cost consequences.

\subsection{Case 2: resolve the WRA versus AHB cover asymmetry}
\paragraph{Two axes of asymmetry: threat polarity and action risk}
WRA's one-decoy $E_{\rm func}$ drops from 0.770 to 0.610, whereas AHB's Trip Planning success falls from 0.75 to 0.15 under \texttt{Online+Fake} humanization \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing}. Grouping both as ``cover traffic'' conceals different objectives and executions.

WRA discards decoy reads for Type~I confidentiality; AHB executes GUI events for Type~III evasion, potentially navigating or submitting forms. Their utility outcomes are not contradictory; AHB's missing condition-specific denominators also preclude a matched causal effect. Their shared workflow setting does not establish shared state effects.

Terminal-state reward and dispatch-time authorization answer different questions: an unconfirmed write can still reach the intended final state. Exp~2 (\S\ref{sec:exp-state}) checks both; the fresh-planner study measures the separate deadline/utility boundary.

\subsection{Case 3: bind adaptive retraining and cost objects}
\paragraph{The $D_0\to D_2$ rebound and component-cost boundaries}
A matched adaptation contrast compares clean-trained $D_0$ and defense-trained $D_2$ on identical defended tests. This follows established adaptive WF evaluation; the rebound itself is not our discovery. UI Traces and Dangol identify the additional record-binding question: whether a refit, measured delay and utility refer to the same executed condition \cite{uitraces2026,dangol2026workflow}. Exp~4 tests that question with timing and matched observation sets.

Early Bird's $6$ms/$4\%$ GPTCache cost does not price $K$-prefix isolation; KVGov's $1.3\,\mu$s HMAC microcost omits lost reuse; SpliceDefense's QCP cost does not price CTBF+QCP \cite{earlybird2025,addagada2026kvgov,spliceleak2026}. CachePrune binds protection, QA/summarization utility and throughput, while OTRO separates conditional leakage from TTFT/padding costs \cite{wu2026cacheprune,lee2026otro}. The 39-record census makes these component substitutions observable without ranking incompatible outcomes.
