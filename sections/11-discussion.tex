\subsection{Illustration: bind the final target-bearing emitter}
NetEcho's upstream packet-size shaping operates on a different projection from the tool-call sequence. Its plaintext-aware frontend then emits a new size trace (grouped token counts or character lengths), reopening a size channel after upstream protection \cite{netecho2025}. SplitDecoy is a separate map-only return-path example, outside the 39-record mitigation ledger: the cloud sees nonzero gradient row support on every tested frame across nine seeds \cite[Sections 5.1--5.3]{politis2026splitdecoy}. Both change the receiver boundary and therefore require a separately bound inference result.

SICC further isolates length/structure-based inference after message content is authorized \cite[Table~3]{xu2026sicc}. DP-MemView's separate diagnostic exposes an internal score while holding compliant view-selection and response paths fixed, yielding an attribute AUC of 0.976 \cite[RQ2, following Table~4]{dpmemview2026}. This is a different released view, not an attack on the compliant interface. The reporting card therefore retains the semantic privacy result and separately rejects applying it to the exposed score.

\subsection{R44: bind utility to the action that executes}
WRA discards decoy reads for Type~I confidentiality; AHB's Type~III evasion executes GUI events, a different objective and state contract \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing}. A preserved response and an unchanged database therefore establish different functional claims. Our fresh retail-sandbox study tests this distinction with separately executed planner/user interactions and cloned initial states; these share tasks and services and are not statistically independent trials. After the first eligible order lookup, the three arms add nothing, one hidden read, or one hidden same-item write. The write's response is discarded, but its payment-ledger and modification-quota effects remain visible to subsequent execution.

A major limitation is the mutable Qwen service alias: its underlying model weights are not pinned. This convenience sample contains 187 three-arm groups covering 98 tasks selected nonrandomly from incomplete GLM blocks. The selected subset retains the protocol's randomized execution order and arm permutations; this does not make the cohort representative. The 113 complete GLM groups remain a separate exploratory description. Averaging each task's available repeats before giving tasks equal weight, write-cover success is 46.9 percentage points below baseline (95\% task-bootstrap interval $[-56.8,-36.9]$). The read-cover difference is $+0.7$ points with interval $[-4.9,6.1]$, so effects from approximately a 5-point loss to a 6-point gain remain consistent with the interval. This demonstrates utility harm in a deliberately state-changing write condition, not the effect of WRA or a general write-cover mechanism. All 97 executed cover writes consume the sandbox's modification eligibility; payment-ledger and quota semantics make the direction of harm unsurprising. Among 115 failed write-arm episodes in this convenience sample, 28 subsequently received the native non-pending error on the same order after the cover consumed eligibility. This narrow recorded marker is not a causal attribution; the other 87 failures have not been adjudicated into planner deviation and other causes, and may still involve quota effects. The original study does not isolate how much of the difference each mechanism causes, test an online side-effect-free write control or estimate a general cost of write cover. Earlier reference-program shadow checks do not provide that missing fresh-planner control. Recovery changed concurrency from 10 to 2 and the transport path; ten interrupted request waits and server outcomes remain unknown. These limits accompany the numerical difference. Appendix~\ref{app:boundary-experiments} retains all three arms, the paired tests and the cohort boundary.

\subsection{E4: bind adaptation, calibration and cost to the tested setting}
The replay study uses three pinned local model artifacts, 100 synthetic topics with five templates each, and a 128-token generation budget. The binary task distinguishes 50 medical from 50 nonmedical topics, with five topic-held-out folds and a 50\% chance balanced accuracy (BA). Saved output chunks are emitted through timed TLS loopback. This finite setting does not establish outcomes for cloud services, real-user topics, WANs or defenses operating during generation.

For each defended test view, $D_0$ fits on clean training data and $D_2$ on defended training data. At Qwen~8B's default four-chunk batching point, a post hoc fixed logistic-regression comparison on the same 500 held-out predictions gives $D_0$ BA/AUROC 0.500/0.678 and $D_2$ 0.732/0.773. The paired differences are 0.232 BA and 0.094 AUROC; topic-stratified bootstrap intervals conditional on the saved predictions are [0.168, 0.286] and [0.005, 0.189], respectively. Five further independently shuffled topic-level five-fold splits give $D_0$ AUROC 0.676--0.751 and $D_2$ AUROC 0.770--0.785. A separate fixed-learner 20-topic holdout gives AUROC 0.758 and 0.807, respectively. These fits use the same defended test topics for both conditions and select no winner on the held-out data, but reuse the already inspected synthetic dataset and are exploratory. The earlier separately selected three-learner maxima also remain exploratory; neither analysis independently evaluates a post-selection research program. AUROC evaluates score ranking across thresholds, whereas BA uses one classification threshold, which can yield chance-level decisions despite informative ranking. The $D_0$ BA is not evidence that the clean-trained attack was defeated. For the same native outputs, raw BA/AUROC are 0.744/0.800; the batched-minus-raw point differences, $-0.012$/$-0.027$, lack difference intervals.

All calibration comparisons are exploratory; the calibration audit also limits the claim. Default bucket, batch and fixed-cell parameters are identical across all source folds, so their zero imported-minus-local differences are degenerate comparisons with no evidence about transferring a changed calibration. Some hard-cap source/fold comparisons have different parameters, while others remain identical. In a new retrospective parameter-level control, 500 class-stratified topic resamples per target and training fold refit the local event-count cap. For Qwen~8B fold~0, the local cap is 87 and its resampled 2.5th--97.5th percentile range is 83--89 events; imported Qwen~0.6B and Llama~1B caps are 90 and 128. This control shows finite-sample variation of the fitted parameter, not a refitted attack or uncertainty in the protection effect. At the reported precision, five of the six original conditional intervals include or touch zero; only Llama~1B to Qwen~8B excludes zero, at $+3.4$ attack-BA points with interval $[0.8,5.4]$. Higher attack BA means worse protection for that imported cap under this comparison, not improved defense utility. The secondary Holm adjustment covers two imports per target, not all targets, policies and points. Appendix~\ref{app:boundary-experiments} reports all six and the omitted fitting and selection uncertainty. The evidence neither establishes a robust transfer effect nor demonstrates equivalence or absence of a source effect.

Cost must follow the same configuration. For those 500 Qwen~8B batching replays, response application TLS bytes total 470,239 versus 1,234,823 for paired raw captures (0.381$\times$), because aggregation reduces framing and record overhead. Paired completion differences have median $+0.152$~ms and 95th percentile $+2.381$~ms; the 95th-percentile delay of original chunk endpoints is $+65.730$~ms. These are local replay measurements over 100 topics, excluding requests and handshakes. They do not price online generation or a WAN. The next deployment measurement is an adapted attacker and a nondegenerate, locally controlled calibration comparison, with inference and cost bound to that same execution. The 500-session whole-arm repeat was post hoc, after an incomplete original run; its recovery used a separate runtime and batch. Matching descriptive summaries do not establish exchangeability or rule out batch effects. The appendix and supplement retain the provenance. No additional topics or semireal corpus are included.

\subsection{Bounded reappraisal of published results}
The external WF examples are retrospective applications with the published outcomes visible, not new experiments or independent predictions; their accounting is in Table~\ref{tab:source-ledger}. Two examples make the assessment inspectable. WFDefProxy's FRONT comparison reports DF TPR 72.06\% versus 42.79\%, FPR 2.16\% versus 2.49\%, and data overhead 52\% versus 75\% \cite[6.2, Table~10]{gong2021wfdefproxy}. The retained assessment keeps each observed protection/cost tuple; it does not attach simulated cost to a live outcome or rank protection at a common FPR. Mathews et al.'s DynaFlow live result reports 379\% mean per-site time overhead, but changed URLs and crawler prevent treating its simulation/live difference as a matched causal effect \cite[IV-F, Table~II; Appendix~B]{mathews2023critical}.

These distinctions were already supported by the cited papers. The LLM application makes the analogous join explicit: Early Bird's approximately 6\,ms semantic-cache de-identification cost does not price its prefix rule, while CachePrune's default-configuration association still does not establish paired protection/serving trials (\S\ref{sec:cache-decisions}). The concrete output is a source-located acceptance or rejection of that particular join, not discovery of a new source-paper error or evidence that analysts using the card outperform existing practice. The full external ledger, including favorable scoped results, remains in the supplement.

\paragraph{What these illustrations add}
The contribution is an auditable organization of source-specific comparisons: which observation a result covers, which state a tool action changes, and which fitting or calibration quantities differ. The source examples show what a reporting card records; E4 and R44 supply bounded measurements of selected cases. They do not establish new general evaluation principles, reliable analyst judgments or improved deployment outcomes. Those claims remain untested.

\subsection{A deployment query determines the evidence to retain}
Start with the intended receiver, protected outcome and intervention (Figure~\ref{fig:main-decision}). Record-level comparison identifies whether the cited result measures that object. Figure~\ref{fig:main-decision}'s supported, incompatible and unresolved outcomes map respectively to the scope-permitted descriptive comparison, a conflicting required object, and a missing binding in Table~\ref{tab:comparison-contracts}; paired effects and transfer require their additional stated conditions. Configuration-level joint binding identifies which inference, utility and cost outcomes share its configuration. Deployment decisions use the holdout matrix to identify evidence for the proposed change and specify the remaining measurement. Table~\ref{tab:r19-decisions} proposes the following evidence requests: a new emitter needs a receiver-specific capture, writes need state/authorization checks, source-calibration comparisons need a local fitting-variability control, and an online scheduler needs its own planner utility and latency measurement. The calibration control follows from the comparison design: changing the source can also change the finite training sample used to fit the defense.
