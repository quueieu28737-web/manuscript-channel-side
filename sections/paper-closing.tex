\section{Ethics and Availability}\label{sec:validity}
\paragraph{Ethics}
Inputs are synthetic, captures are restricted to the experiment, and state-stress writes affect isolated fixtures. The hosted pilot preserved in the evidence archive used account-holder authorization; provider research terms remain unverified, and it supports none of the present main results. Traffic inference can enable profiling; our measurements target synthetic tickets. AI tools assisted extraction, implementation and writing; observations and analyses are preserved. Credentials, hosted transcripts and captures, and third-party full texts are excluded from distribution.

\paragraph{Availability}
The accompanying review artifact provides manuscript sources, versioned collection and analysis implementations, fixture generation, frozen plans and historical timing pools, aggregate tests and costs, the ideal-reference computation, and the source-located background matrix. Its index distinguishes arithmetic checks from full-data replication. Released aggregates support paired-test arithmetic and reference enumeration. Per-episode records, individual timestamps and predictions, captures and TLS keys remain local. Independent refitting, feature reconstruction and per-fixture role checks require those withheld inputs. No public release of them is promised.

\section{Conclusion}
Cover generation and execution must be evaluated together. In the measured Agent loop, controller insertion bypasses a model wait and produces a removable short-gap signature when calls are delimited. Sampled waits defeat that shortcut while mixed-context attacks retain task recognition. With inferred events, the prespecified removal pipeline loses its advantage. Complete shadows lower measured recognition at the cost of more calls and model work; the comparison does not isolate online generation. Recording inference, goals, business state and cost on matched executions connects these traffic results to the authority and resources a policy consumes.
