\section{Controlled Studies Supporting the Synthesis}\label{sec:controlled-placement}\label{sec:tau-cover-bridge}
Four studies attach measured consequences to the evidence-reuse decisions of \S\ref{sec:discussion}: emitter, executed state, calibration and attacker observation. Exps~1--2 reproduce mechanisms and measure their costs; Exps~3--4 examine agent operation signatures, training variability and execution conditions. Populations remain distinct (Table~\ref{tab:r7-cohorts}). Supplement S31 indexes the detailed operating grids; Appendix~\ref{app:experiments} indexes the studies.

\paragraph{Measurement contract}
A transparent relay captures post-handshake TLS application records on loopback. BA averages class recalls; reported BA is the maximum over the named attack suite. Intervals reselect that maximum over 2,000 task- or customer-cluster bootstrap draws. The frozen size/order suite uses clean-trained and defended-refit learners plus decoder/count rules; the endpoint suite adds total-exchange count. Cross-fit/localization use an 11-attack union; timing uses a 17--22-attack union, with seven shared learners for offline controls. R21 additionally resamples calibration and attacker training. The protocol's absolute screen (upper 95\% BA below 0.60 and AUROC below 0.65 for both targets) remains unmet in Exps~3--4; the comparisons below measure scoped inference and cost.

\subsection{Exp 1: emitter changes require new inference and cost evidence}\label{sec:exp-emitter}
For 48 saved brief/expanded responses on 24 topics (12 training, 12 test), padding only the upstream link leaves verbosity 100\% separable after plaintext-aware re-emission, reproducing NetEcho's mechanism \cite{netecho2025}. A buffered training cap reaches 50\% BA at 2.18/2.19$\times$ bytes under TLS~1.2/1.3. Streaming the same texts per token changes both the observable and cost: eight-token batching gives 50\% clean-trained BA but 100\% after defended refitting; constant event size and count reach 50\% at 2.42$\times$ bytes and 208 versus 105 token intervals, with two test overflows \cite{weiss2024prompt,whisperleak2025}. \textbf{Reuse decision:} a buffered cap cannot price streaming protection; capture and fit at the final emitter, including overflow and delivery delay.

\subsection{Exp 2: read cover does not bind write-state preservation}\label{sec:exp-state}
Across 97 initially eligible retail tasks and three seeds, same-item modification changes status and appends a refund in 173 of 291 placements; 118 fail availability checks. An idempotent operation can still change the original state. Shadow execution isolates cover effects and suppresses responses; the live-cancellation control changes the database in every case. These conformance checks establish the chosen state boundary.

On all 115 reference programs, task-folded count/order/length attacks recover 100\% BA after refitting to a single cancellation decoy. Equalizing the target count leaves 76.9\%; equalizing the full tool shape reaches 50.0/54.5\% cancel/return BA at 6.71 extra calls per real call. The 4,140-session TLS composition test then shows why dispatch and transport are separate: per-call buckets leave return BA at 73.9\%, whereas joint cover/framing reaches 54.4/50.0\% at 26.3$\times$ bytes and 7.7$\times$ calls. \textbf{Reuse decision:} bind cover to its executed state and observed link; a passive-link tunnel can replace tool-executing cover at that link, but dispatch protection after decapsulation requires isolation (\S\ref{sec:deployment}).

\subsection{Exp 3: separate operation inference, calibration and utility}\label{sec:real-agent}\label{sec:r5}
\paragraph{Frozen operating point}
GPT-6.1 Sol/low executes 115 retail tasks with two prompts and three repeats: 690 runs from 53 customers. Targets are the requested cancellation/return operations in the reference program. The rules prompt adds reads before order access and writes. All 414 training runs precede freezing cover donors, templates and framing caps; 276 test runs cover 46 tasks and 24 disjoint customers. Prefix cover flushes unmet counts at episode end. Replaying each trajectory under four program conditions per prompt yields 2,760 local TLS captures; real responses and live state are preserved by construction (Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence}).

Rules improve read-order adherence from 86.2\% to 97.1\% while undefended inference remains above 93\%: policy compliance and operation privacy separate. Joint shaping reduces standard-prompt BA from 97.2/94.0\% to 50.0/50.0\%; upper bounds are 60.6/65.5\%. \paragraph{Localization and model-specific evaluation}
Writes are 19.6\% of real calls yet retain oracle-selected 91.7/84.9\% BA; removing them leaves 62.8/59.9\%. This locates the metadata signature of an operation-defined target and motivates testing write-local intervention. On MiniMax/GLM, write-only padding costs 1.45/1.51$\times$ bytes but leaves return BA at 61.3/61.6\%. A learned write detector has precision 34.9/98.3\% and recall 96.3/89.4\%, so oracle localization and implementable inference have different scopes.

\paragraph{Calibration source versus local training variability}

\paragraph{Planner utility at the executed scheduler}
A paired 50-task GLM study runs 100 fresh episodes with isolated prefix-cover execution and emulated response deadlines. Baseline/prefix-shadow succeed on 37/50 and 33/50 tasks (difference $-8.0$ points, customer interval $[-23.9,6.5]$). Withheld executed-tool responses trigger later planner actions. This measures the prefix scheduler's utility; the replayed joint-framing/terminal-flush mechanism needs its own live utility measurement. \textbf{Reuse decision:} retain logical response/state conformance and measure deadline/replanning utility at the actual scheduler.
