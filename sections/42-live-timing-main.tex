\paragraph{Expand the observation on matched captures}
Adding timing/flush attacks to size/order attacks increases serial-shadow cancellation BA from 50.0\% to 61.4\% (paired $+11.4$ points, interval $[3.7,19.6]$); return changes from 50.7\% to 53.4\%. Flush detection reaches 90.4/92.3\% for serial/parallel schedules; composing a learned suffix detector with operation inference yields a strongest return point of 60.7\%. The full union retains stripped and unstripped traces. Thus recognizable cover phases and downstream operation recovery are measured separately.

\paragraph{Retain the cost object when comparing mechanisms}
Measured RPC conditions show a cost--inference trade-off: per-call constant framing uses 3.92$\times$ bytes and 16.6\,ms p95 at 68.6/62.7\% full-union BA, versus serial shadow's 15.11$\times$, 26.2\,ms and 61.4/53.4\%. Offline random-donor WT reaches 50.0/56.7\% at 2.04$\times$ under the common seven, identifying a lower-byte candidate for online evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:r7-controls} keeps its transformed metadata and simulated span separate from measured RPC latency and the extended timing suite.

\paragraph{Size the transport horizon to the receiving path}
Re-running all 276 test episodes under 20/5 and 50/10\,ms one-way delay/jitter retains 89.4\% flush-detector BA versus 90.4\% locally, while serial-shadow cancellation inference falls to 50.0/54.0\%. The 2\,ms tunnel's 266\,ms horizon completes only 85/276 and 5/276 sessions; shadow/per-call framing complete all, with serial-shadow p95 of 855.4/1,999.5\,ms. \textbf{Reuse decision:} expand the attacker's observation on matched captures, measure online latency for the chosen mechanism, and reprice a horizon calibrated for the target path. Together these studies supply operating examples for the SoK's decision table.
