\paragraph{Match cost to the protected boundary}
Within measured RPC conditions (M), per-call constant framing uses 3.92$\times$ bytes and 16.6\,ms p95, versus serial shadow's 15.11$\times$ and 26.2\,ms; its full-union BA is higher (68.6/62.7\% versus 61.4/53.4\%). This establishes a measured cost--inference trade-off. Measured real-chain p95 latency is 26.2/20.8\,ms for serial/two-in-flight shadow, versus 56.4/112.3\,ms for 2/5\,ms tunnels. Sessions preserve real responses and final live state; fixed tunnel sizes/counts still expose host-scheduled direction/order. Table~\ref{tab:r7-controls} keeps measured RPC times separate from offline schedule transformations.

Under the common seven attacks, offline random-donor Walkie-Talkie (WT, S) reaches 50.0/56.7\% BA at 2.04$\times$ bytes; measured serial shadow (M) reaches 50.0/52.9\% at 15.11$\times$. This descriptive, shared-metadata comparison identifies a lower-byte shaping candidate. Establishing its online trade-off requires RPC measurements and the extended timing/flush suite; the offline point is neither a theoretical byte lower bound nor demonstrated deployment superiority.

The WF adapters use the same saved traffic and training customers. WT samples donor traces without symmetric site pairing; WTF-PAD samples training inter-arrival histograms; Tamaraw and FRONT supply fixed-rate and front-loaded controls. Input-derived fragments preserve sizes, directions and order, enabling a controlled metadata comparison. Their simulated spans and measured RPC latency remain separate cost objects.

\paragraph{Robustness under WAN transit delay and jitter}
Because local loopback exhibits zero transit jitter, we repeat the evaluation on all 276 test episodes under network emulation matching wide-area links (20\,ms delay with 5\,ms jitter, and 50\,ms delay with 10\,ms jitter).
In the supplementary WAN evaluation, flush-phase detection remains remarkably robust: the frozen loopback detector $D_{\text{loop}}$ transfers directly to WAN traffic with 89.4\% BA and 0.962--0.963 AUROC (versus 90.4\% and 0.967 on loopback), and locally adapted retraining yields 89.0--89.2\% BA. The macroscopic density transition of dummy cover requests is largely preserved across transit variance.
Concurrently, transit jitter degrades the attacker's fine-grained intent inference: under serial shadow cover, full-suite cancellation BA drops from 61.4\% on loopback to 50.0\% (chance) under 20\,ms/5\,ms jitter and 54.0\% under 50\,ms/10\,ms jitter.
Crucially, WAN emulation exposes an operational failure boundary for fixed-rate tunnels: while loopback completed 276/276 sessions within the fixed 133-slot (266\,ms) horizon, network round-trip delays exhaust the slot budget for multi-call agent workflows before all real tool responses return, failing 69.2\% (85/276 completed) and 98.2\% (5/276 completed) of sessions. In contrast, shadow cover and constant framing preserve 100\% correctness and live state fidelity, while their real-chain p95 latency expands proportionally to the serial sum of network RTTs (855.4\,ms and 1999.5\,ms).

\textbf{Finding.} A length-only verdict misses schedule leakage. The common outcome is suite-maximum BA, always indexed by its observed features and fitted attacker set: Exp~3\textquotesingle s 50.0/50.0\% and Exp~4\textquotesingle s 61.4/53.4\% cross an attacker-observation boundary and are not interchangeable protection estimates. Within M, per-call framing costs fewer bytes and less RPC time than serial shadow but leaves higher full-union inference; S identifies lower-byte candidates under the common seven attacks. For a passive ingress-link observer, lightweight shaping is a relevant candidate; for a restricted dispatch observer, execution isolation addresses an additional state boundary. This is the audit's placement-and-cost distinction demonstrated on one workload.
