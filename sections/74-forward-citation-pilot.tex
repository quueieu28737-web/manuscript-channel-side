\section{Forward-Citation Context Pilot}\label{sec:forward-pilot}
We inspected whether citing sources transfer Early Bird's approximately 6\,ms/4\% semantic-cache de-identification cost to a different prefix defense, or KVGov's approximately $1.3\,\mu$s HMAC microcost to a complete defended deployment. A positive finding requires a source attribution and numerical transfer to a different configuration or cost object. Citations that do not use the number cannot establish this kind of misuse.

On 27 October 2026 a convenience search located five citing works and six checked citation edges: KVGov, Time Will Tell, PrefixWall and an autonomous-driving roadmap cite Early Bird; an LLM contention study cites both Early Bird and KVGov \cite{addagada2026kvgov,timewilltell2024}. Primary contexts and locators are listed in the released pilot ledger, including \url{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06853v1}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10726} and \url{https://doi.org/10.1145/3807455}. No target cost transfer was found in the inspected passages. KVGov's Early Bird citation has a version/metric ambiguity that is not sufficient to establish an error. One roadmap passage was obtained from an indexed excerpt rather than a complete PDF; its full context is unresolved.

An OpenAlex metadata query resolved identifiers for all 20 families of the extended inventory and returned eight indexed citing works: seven for the merged Early Bird preprint/journal record and one for a remote-timing paper. The index returned none for KVGov, while a separately located paper cites it. Seven of the eight indexed contexts remain unretrieved; they are unknown, not negative cases. After merging with targeted search, 12 candidate or verified citing works are retained; five have inspected passages. The index is incomplete, the pilot is not a random sample, and no misuse rate is estimated. A proposed deployment comparison remains an example of a measurement decision, rather than an observed attribution error.
