\section{Introduction}
An Agent receives the instruction \emph{process the next support ticket}. It reads the ticket, checks an order and approval, consults a model, and submits a refund. Encryption hides message contents from a network observer, but the contacted services, traffic sizes and timing remain visible. The tool's answer then becomes part of a model request, which creates a second observable representation of the same task. Suppose a defender adds cover traffic. A second question follows: does that cover end at a proxy, execute a read, or change business state? Figure~\ref{fig:chain} follows information and actions through this example.

\paragraph{Why systematize now}
Agent deployments are converging on a small set of protocols, notably the Model Context Protocol (MCP) over HTTP/2 or HTTP/3, and streamed model APIs~\cite{mcp2025transports,rfc9113}. In under three years, studies have recovered research queries from contacted domains~\cite{jeong2026wra}, recognized tasks from model traffic~\cite{li2026ant,pouryousef2026traffic}, fingerprinted hosted Agents~\cite{agentprint2025} and attributed models from tool-call behavior~\cite{zhao2026agenttrace,ediga2026trace}. The first defenses are research decoys, relays and shaping~\cite{jeong2026wra,dangol2026workflow}. Most of this work is preprint-stage, and no shared evaluation standard exists. Website fingerprinting (WF) went through the same phase, and many early attack and defense results were later reassessed under realistic populations and adapted attackers~\cite{dyer2012peekaboo,juarez2014critical,mathews2023critical}. An SoK at this point can set evaluation expectations before results pile up that cannot be compared. Its corpus is necessarily small, so we make every conclusion's source dependence explicit.

\paragraph{The question}
Which established traffic-analysis evaluation conditions carry over to Agent execution, and which ones break? We find that two assumptions underlying WF-style defense evaluation do not hold, and each produces a concrete evaluation failure.

\textbf{Finding 1: the unit of protection is a set of links, not one trace.} WRA recovers research queries from tool destinations, while ANT and a cloud benchmark recognize task labels on model connections~\cite{jeong2026wra,li2026ant,pouryousef2026traffic}. A contacted tool additionally reads its arguments, and PrivacyTrace shows that these arguments raise attribute inference well above a schema prior~\cite{duan2026privacytrace}. Because tool results are forwarded into later model requests, a tool-side defense leaves the model link outside its scope. In our forwarding pilot, removing supplementary records at the first feedback step still leaves complete-execution model-view recognition at 44/120 for Qwen and 33/120 for Llama, where chance is 20/120 (Section~\ref{sec:live-study}). A defense claim must therefore name every view its attacker retains (Section~\ref{sec:authorization}).

\textbf{Finding 2: executed cover makes utility part of the privacy result.} Classical cover terminates at a tunnel endpoint~\cite{apthorpe2019stp}. An Agent decoy can execute queries or update state. The published research-decoy result lowers query recovery from .770 to .565, but its utility scores belong to a separate destination-visibility intervention~\cite{jeong2026wra}. Across the four mapped query/workflow privacy settings, adapted attacks appear in two and matched task utility in one, and no setting reports both (Section~\ref{sec:reporting}). The field therefore has no privacy--utility operating point for any Agent defense.

\textbf{Finding 3: in a controlled test, cover semantics change state but not privacy.} We test six cover policies on 1,440 paired held-out LLM-planned executions over MCP Streamable HTTP (Section~\ref{sec:cover-study}). For Qwen3:8B, proxy-discarded, read, isolated and live-write cover leave the validation-selected attacker at 61--62/120 (no cover: 118/120). State integrity, however, ranges from 116/120 to 7/120. A simple last-destination rule (77/120) beats the learned attacker because the frozen schedule leaves the end of the trace uncovered. The resulting design rule is to terminate cover as early as the observer model allows, and to spend the budget on schedule coverage rather than on execution depth. The live-write arm is a deliberately adverse control; it measures what a completion-only metric misses, not how often deployed cover causes harm.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An access-aware evidence map} of 44 settings from 15 Agent studies. It separates transit, recipient and active observers and binds each score to its target, population and supplied boundaries (Sections~\ref{sec:scope}--\ref{sec:taxonomy}, Tables~\ref{tab:deployment-access}--\ref{tab:attack-recipient}).
\item \textbf{A classical-to-Agent transfer matrix} that labels each established WF and traffic-analysis condition as retained, extended, untested or mismatched in access (Section~\ref{sec:correspondence}).
\item \textbf{Two execution-aware syntheses}, of feedback exposure and of cover termination, with a design space of six defense layers (Sections~\ref{sec:evidence}--\ref{sec:defenses}).
\item \textbf{Controlled measurements}: the executed-cover study and a forwarding/concurrency pilot. They supply Finding 3 and calibrate how future Agent defenses should be measured (Section~\ref{sec:experiment}).
\item \textbf{A four-step evaluation procedure, an eight-field checklist and a source-withdrawal analysis} that shows which conclusions survive with only published sources (Sections~\ref{sec:reporting}--\ref{sec:agenda}).
\end{itemize}
