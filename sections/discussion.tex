\section{Synthesis and Research Agenda}\label{sec:agenda}
\textbf{The field map turns a privacy score into a protection decision by binding its observations to executable work.}
The strongest cross-study consequence is intervention matching: WRA's visibility utility cannot establish executed-decoy utility, and adapted OHTTP inference with traffic cost does not establish original-task preservation. The technical case adds the dispatcher: controller cover can omit model waits and become removable, while planner cover incurs model work. These connections extend the evaluation object; the underlying adaptive-attack and state-correctness principles are established.

\subsection{Source dependence}\label{sec:sensitivity}
The evidence map follows 33 statements in seven comparisons across 20 source-removal scenarios (Supplement S4.4). Strictly retaining inspected published texts leaves WRA's tool-destination recovery and decoy/visibility distinction, plus CHeaT's defined detection/prevention endpoints. The model-view observations, live OHTTP refitting and each named Agent transfer axis depend on preprints. The local cover case adds implementation-specific evidence, not independent confirmation of those source results. This early SoK is useful for fixing interpretation and evaluation conditions; the small direct corpus limits field-wide empirical conclusions.

\subsection{Tests that would change the decision}
Table~\ref{tab:open-questions} gives discriminating tests rather than a list of desirable benchmarks. Priority tests are equal-budget cover schedules under production state contracts; independent role/chain attacks on planner-generated cover; discovery and inference on continuous workloads; and length/path-controlled feedback interventions. Business invariants should admit legitimate concurrent updates. Future evidence can support a policy by meeting declared inference and utility thresholds, or reject it by exposing a budget or state violation.

\section{Validity, Ethics and Availability}\label{sec:validity}
\textbf{The conclusions apply to the recorded sources and declared observation conditions.}
Coverage combines bounded retrieval with citation tracing; failed indices limit recall. Most direct sources are preprints. The independent coder's decision-critical subset agrees on the four-setting privacy denominator and its adaptation/utility fields; other extracted fields do not inherit that reliability estimate.

The live case uses synthetic local tools, two fixed 8B models and reused fixtures/phrasings. The original analysis supplies call windows; the new record-only analysis retains episode-associated connections but supplies no call boundaries. Same-service calls serialize. This does not test unknown-task discovery, unrestricted multiplexing or a remote deployment. Minimum historical holds do not ensure achieved timing distributions match. Persistent caches and phase order affect costs. Intervals condition on the fitted attacks and workload; test maxima are descriptive, not all-attacker bounds. Shadow's identical selected predictors do not establish role indistinguishability. The separate forwarding pilot remains inconclusive and changes content, length and order together.

\paragraph{Ethics and assistance}
All experimental inputs are synthetic; capture filters restrict collection to the experiment. The adverse-write control runs on isolated fixtures. No customers or production business states are affected. An earlier hosted retest used account-holder authorization; provider research terms remain unverified and its results support no main claim. AI assisted extraction, implementation and writing; measurements are backed by saved observations and executable analysis. Traffic inference can enable profiling. Credentials, transcripts, raw captures, model weights and third-party full texts are excluded from distribution.

\paragraph{Availability}
The source release contains the paper, generated aggregate tables and analysis code. The forty-four-setting extraction and source-locator sheets accompany the local review package. Complete individual record metadata and predictions remain in the author's local package; they are not claimed publicly available. Rebuilding the new raw-record inputs needs the retained private records. Aggregate tables, complete configurations and saved-verification scopes provide the alternative checking path.

\section{Conclusion}
Executed cover creates both network observations and application work. This SoK maps the existing evidence to those two outputs and provides a joint protocol for residual inference, task completion, business state and resource cost. The live case shows how a controller's dispatch process exposes cover, how historical and planner-generated schedules change the measured attacks, and why a goal score alone cannot certify the whole execution. Protection claims become actionable when their privacy and business evidence describe the same policy on the same workload.
