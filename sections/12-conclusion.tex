\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The inspected LLM/agent mitigation sources report inference outcomes more consistently than utility or matched adaptive evaluation. This pattern survives accepting partial bindings and restricting outcomes to a common family population. Agent privacy/attribution studies provide partial usefulness evidence but no explicit task-utility binding in the four-family subset. Five of eight families with explicit inference and cost place the two in different records; the operating point determines whether they can be combined.

The SoK connects these reporting patterns to deployment decisions through scoped result records. Positive packages such as CachePrune and SparLeak show what can be retained, while the holdout synthesis identifies whether a changed system has attack-side or defense-side evidence. The released record identities, sensitivity checks and filled reporting cards make the findings inspectable and turn the remaining bindings into concrete measurement requirements.
