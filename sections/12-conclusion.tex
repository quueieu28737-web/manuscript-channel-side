\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The proposed reporting card turns a reuse question into an inspectable decision: retain a source-scoped comparison, identify an incompatible binding, or specify a missing measurement. It links each result to the receiver, affected execution state, attacker fitting and workload, while keeping protection, function and cost distinct. In the synthetic E4 replay, changing the attacker's training from clean to defended outputs changes measured balanced accuracy from 0.500 to 0.732; in the separate retail-sandbox convenience sample, state-changing write cover is associated with a 46.9-point decrease in task-weighted success. These cases illustrate why attacker adaptation and action effects matter when interpreting evidence. The card remains unvalidated, and the selected cases do not establish a general mitigation effect or deployment benefit.
