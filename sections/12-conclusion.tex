\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
In the inspected privacy/attribution mitigation sources, explicit inference bindings are more frequent than explicit function or matched fitting bindings under the selected rules. Accepting partial function evidence changes the count from 3/16 to 5/16. The four-family agent subset and the record-splitting sensitivity motivate source-specific decisions rather than general prevalence claims.

The SoK connects these patterns to deployment decisions through source-linked results. CachePrune and SparLeak illustrate what can be retained at a measured configuration; the holdout synthesis distinguishes attack-side evidence from defense transfer. The decision procedure identifies the receiving view, execution state and evaluation condition before naming the result to retain or the measurement still needed.
