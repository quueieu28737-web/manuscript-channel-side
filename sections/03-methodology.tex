\section{Sources and Reporting Cards}\label{sec:methodology}\label{sec:background}
\subsection{Selection frames and focused source follow-up}
The original map is a purposefully assembled set of example material, closed on October~21, 2026; its nested counts are retained in Table~\ref{tab:source-ledger} and the supplement. Core inclusion requires LLM/agent execution (C1), a hidden property (C2), an execution-dependent auxiliary observable (C3), specified observer permissions (C4) and an empirical inference evaluation (C5). Adjacent families remain contextual entries rather than cases satisfying all five conditions. Ordinary response semantics alone do not satisfy the auxiliary-observable condition. The resulting privacy/attribution mitigation sample is therefore selected through attack evaluation, not a census of defense literature. It includes both attack-oriented and defense-oriented studies.

A defense-first exercise on October~27 examines a purposive challenge set: four named seeds (CacheSolidarity, PlanTwin, SICC and DP-MemView) and two selected additions (OCELOT and AirGapAgent) from nine logged searches. Prior reconnaissance and the four-seed/two-addition cap are recorded, but the retained protocol has no independently verifiable preregistration timestamp. The exercise tests source-specific scope interpretations; it does not correct the original selection bias or supply a representative defense sample. The cards distinguish auxiliary timing/form/score observations from semantic transcript and information-flow protection. Source versions, search returns, admission decisions and unassessed candidates are retained in the supplement.

The original search uses seeds, venue-targeted screening, citation tracing, Semantic Scholar and native arXiv queries. The frozen map and query dispositions remain available for reconstruction. Neither it nor the challenge exercise systematically covers architecture/hardware venues such as ISCA, MICRO, HPCA, ASPLOS and CHES/TCHES. Formal guarantees, component costs and empirical recovery scores can answer different questions; requiring a scored auxiliary-channel attack also excludes some useful defense evidence. Privacy and attribution are the primary protected interests; evasion and authorized diagnostics remain boundary comparisons rather than pooled privacy defenses.

The selected SecEmb, Palermo, OTRO, Opal and TDXRay examples illustrate kinds of hardware or formal evidence that C1--C5 can omit; they are not a hardware or formal-methods coverage sample. The retrospective R47 examples use an eligibility rule broader than C5. Direct defenses (D) explicitly propose, implement, formally analyze or evaluate confidentiality or unlinkability against an auxiliary observation in LLM inference, serving or agent execution; neither an empirical attack-success result nor a particular R1--R9 binding is required. Relevant generic CPU/GPU, trusted execution environment (TEE), oblivious RAM (ORAM), constant-time and traffic mechanisms without explicit LLM/agent analysis remain mechanism background (M). Semantic-content privacy, general model safety and secondary discovery material without that auxiliary-observation claim remain adjacent (A); unresolved cases stay pending. Protected interests are recorded separately: user/task privacy, model/service secrets, attribution and audit/detection integrity do not share an effectiveness denominator.

Search and acquisition are frozen at the evidence already recorded for this revision. The protocol's discovery interval is January~1, 2018 through October~30, 2026, 13:15:46 UTC, with earlier foundations retained as background; later readings do not establish public availability at that cutoff. Partial venue inventories, capped index returns and selected-section readings leave coverage, version/family identity and final eligibility incomplete. Earlier results and reviews were visible during this nonblind, model-assisted work. It was neither preregistered nor independently human-coded. Selected sources support bounded comparisons, not a complete defense corpus or field-wide rates. The frozen C1--C5 denominators and historical codes remain unchanged.

\subsection{Source-bound coding and scope}
The retained R1--R9 codes and comparison decisions are the recorded outputs of the disclosed source-coding process; they do not imply independent human coding. Throughout, an unreported item means not located in the inspected version and sections, not proven absent from the source as a whole. We use \emph{reporting card} for the proposed form, \emph{source record} for an inspected source, and \emph{result record} for a specific evaluated configuration and outcome.
R47 source interpretations and unresolved eligibility decisions remain subject to author review before finalization. Each card binds a source version and section/table/figure locator to its target, receiver, intervention, attacker/test condition, functional outcome and cost. Recorded revisions preserve the reason for a scope or applicability change. B denotes an explicit binding, P partial evidence, U unreported in the inspected scope, ? unverified, and -- inapplicable to the requested outcome. Deterministic checks verify counts and membership, not the correctness of source interpretation. Selected second source readings provide bounded computational cross-checks; source locators make the interpretations inspectable.
No independent human-coded agreement estimate or controlled comparison of analysts using this procedure versus existing guidance was collected. Retrospective applications with visible published outcomes cannot estimate decision accuracy or incremental analyst benefit.

The supplement's \emph{Result-record index} and \emph{Current-code reconstruction} retain every record, locator and current code. Publication status and the inspected version remain distinct: a preprint contributes source-scoped evidence, and an artifact's advertised result is not a measured result. Bibliographic syntax or identifier shape does not authenticate a source; unresolved identity, access or version equivalence remains pending in the saved records.
