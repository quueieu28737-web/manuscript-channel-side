\subsection{Deployment and reporting implications}\label{sec:deployment}
Bind each result to its receiving view. Access-network operators, VPC monitors and receiving tools expose different observations; remote paths can reveal destinations even with Encrypted ClientHello \cite{rfc9849}. Ingress evidence does not automatically cover host instrumentation or downstream logs.

Bind state effects to the executed intervention. Read queries, isolated execution and gateway cover have different contracts. Dry-run, sandboxes and rollback have limits \cite{kubernetesdryrun,stripesandboxes,postgresrollback}; idempotence alone is insufficient, and sequence changes can survive rollback \cite{postgressequences}. Record the trust boundary, authorization, quotas and protected outcome with each result.

\paragraph{Unmeasured validity}
Analyst benefit remains untested. A future study could recruit analysts and randomly assign cases and guidance conditions: the proposed card versus established evaluation guidance, with counterbalanced case order and a common time budget. Independent assessors, blinded to condition, would use a prespecified rubric to score source-grounded reuse decisions and missing or incorrect bindings. Report inter-coder agreement, decision accuracy against an adjudicated reference, completion time and uncertainty, with participant and case variation accounted for. Pilot the rubric and register the protocol before collecting outcomes; disagreements and inaccessible sources remain visible. This is a proposed study, not work already performed. Stronger deployment claims need fixed models, a justified cohort, additional domains and a side-effect-free write control for R44. E4 needs independent or nested attacker selection and a complete local calibration-variability control, including replay and attacker refitting at resampled parameters.

\subsection{Limitations and validity}\label{sec:validity}
The reporting card is a proposal, and neither independent coding reliability nor analyst benefit has been measured. The source records are purposefully selected and contain unresolved access, identity and version questions; they do not establish field-wide frequencies. E4 uses a finite synthetic corpus and previously inspected captures, so its new held-out splits are independent of fitting but not of the research history. The local cap resampling does not rerun the attacker. R44's original convenience sample uses a mutable model alias and a single state-changing write mechanism; it lacks an online side-effect-free write control. These constraints bound the respective results, not just their presentation.

\subsection{Ethics and artifact scope}\label{sec:ethics}
The audit uses public research sources. Evasion and research-intent privacy have different authorization relations \cite{zhu2026turing,jeong2026wra}; published attack methods may enable inference. The source-linked descriptions support defensive evaluation but can also help an adversary choose an observation surface; release should omit sensitive traces, credentials and operational misuse details while preserving aggregate evidence. The case study uses cloned benchmark databases, simulated users and controlled sockets, without modifying production accounts. Reconstruction materials include source locators, derived labels, protocols, code and aggregate results. Privacy, data-rights and service-term decisions for traces and third-party material remain unresolved; public access does not grant redistribution permission.

\paragraph{Generative AI use}
AI tools supported research preparation, implementation, source reconciliation and prose. OpenAI GPT-6 Codex assisted the current revision, including analysis code, citation-inventory preparation and prose; the complete earlier assistant-model history is not independently recoverable. Authors remain responsible for the audit codes and interpretations. The E4 streams used the pinned Qwen and Llama local model artifacts identified in the experiment inventory; the original R44 simulated conversations used the mutable Qwen service alias, whose weights were not pinned. Reconstructing analyses requires the specified archived inputs, model/trace identities, code and configuration hashes, environment and saved predictions; replay is not new task-utility evidence. Fresh execution also needs the stated weights or qualified mutable alias, service access and cloned sandbox state. Protocols retain hardware, budgets and recovery records. Missing access or redistribution rights limits reproducibility.
