\paragraph{Cross-layer synthesis}
A remote network observer, a same-site script and a privileged diagnostic hook expose different views even when they share an attribution target. WRA's read-only privacy decoys, AHB's stateful evasion and SplitDecoy's exposed return path illustrate why receiving permissions and protected objectives must accompany the generating layer \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing,politis2026splitdecoy}.

Layer incidence highlights where the systematization's comparison problem arises. In the 58-family map, signal-generation labels cluster at P2 serving/runtime (32 families) and P5 orchestration (19); receiving views cluster at P4 application/API (18), P1 hardware (16), and P3 transport (14). Generation at P3/P4 is rare (one family each), despite frequent observation at those layers. This source/receiver asymmetry makes the transfer question concrete: protection at a frequent source layer cannot be presumed to cover a different receiving projection. The map records layer sets, so it does not assign a causal edge to each source/receiver pair.

Purpose labels further show why a single “agent side channel” category is insufficient: Type I appears 41 times, Type II 13, Type III 2, and Type IV 5. The 32 map-only families extend coverage beyond mitigation comparisons: 15 list serving origins, 11 hardware and 11 orchestration; 19 concern confidentiality, nine attribution, one evasion and all five authorized-diagnostic families. Mapping these families locates privacy targets, operator capabilities and candidate validation environments even when no scoped mitigation record enters the ledger. Counts are overlapping family-label incidences; the audit then applies its reuse rules to the 39 scoped result records.

\subsection{Relationship to prior systematizations}
Prior surveys map compound-system threats, TEE boundaries and privacy contracts \cite{banerjee2024compound,forough2026secrets,kotevska2026privacyfoundations}. WF already supplies feature leakage analysis, cost trade-offs and environment-shift tests \cite{cai2014systematic,juarez2014critical,juarez2016wtfpad,wang2017walkietalkie}. Mathews et al. re-evaluate nine defenses with stronger attacks and an on-network DynaFlow implementation \cite{mathews2023critical}. Adaptive attacks and online-cost evaluation are established foundations.

\paragraph{Adaptive evaluation and benchmarking}
Tram\`er et al. show why defense-specific adaptive attacks require more than applying an unchanged attack; Carlini et al. set out robustness-evaluation practices; RobustBench standardizes the evaluated threat and test interface \cite{tramer2020adaptive,carlini2019evaluating,croce2021robustbench}. These works already motivate protected-data evaluation and reproducible benchmarks. Our R4/R5 items instantiate that discipline. The LLM/agent-specific question is whether a refit, cost or functional result belongs to the same prefix policy, content-aware emitter or state-changing action under discussion.

\paragraph{What this synthesis adds}
Our unit is a \emph{scoped result}, so execution mode, protected state and cost remain attached when one paper reports several mechanisms. The 39-record atlas documents these distinct measurement objects; the 82-family holdout matrix separates evidence for each transfer axis. This connects evidence that earlier taxonomies organize by attack or mechanism: one family can contain several execution modes, a receiving projection can cross system layers, and utility/cost can belong to a different operating point from inference. The synthesis preserves these distinctions when translating a reported result into a deployment decision.

\paragraph{Formal guarantees and runtime measurements}
The mitigation inventory covers evaluated runtime results, not the complete literature on privacy proofs. Differential privacy constrains releases for a specified adjacency relation and privacy budget \cite{dwork2014foundations}; information-flow measures distinguish mutual information from single-guess vulnerability \cite{smith2009qif}. Constant-time verification and oblivious memory protect stated traces under their own leakage models \cite{almeida2016constanttime,stefanov2013pathoram}. These guarantees can supply stronger source-scoped evidence than a low empirical attack score. Their scope still matters: a guarantee for model weights need not include a serving trace, and hiding addresses need not hide access counts. TDXRay and OTRO connect these foundations to the indexed LLM components. For reuse, retain the stated adjacency, leakage model and execution assumptions. Attack-refitting items are inapplicable to a purely formal proof; absence of a classifier experiment does not count against that guarantee.
