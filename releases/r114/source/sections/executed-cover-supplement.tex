\section{LLM-planned executed cover}\label{supp:cover-study}
The experiment measures traffic recognition, requested-task completion and full business-state integrity on the same executions. It uses six synthetic ticket actions and six policies: no cover, proxy discard, read cover, hidden isolated execution, live writes and visible isolated execution. All policies share fixtures and frozen random cover draws. The earlier reference-program replay remains a separate artifact; its fixed-position and one-to-four-decoy sweeps are not LLM results.

\subsection{Population, planner and cover semantics}
Each of two fixed-weight models, Qwen3:8B and Llama3.1:8B, runs 300 fixtures per condition: 120 training, 60 validation and 120 test, balanced across six requested actions. This gives 3,600 episodes, including 1,440 test executions of 120 unique paired test fixtures. Request phrasings recur across splits; this is fixture holdout, not unseen-template transfer. The seed pairs fixtures and decoy schedules, and condition order rotates. Two workers collect each model cohort on one host.

LangGraph~\cite{langchain2025langgraph} alternates model decisions with actual tool calls. The controller fetches the ticket; required checks constrain action admissibility without naming the correct action or object. The model chooses check order, requested business operation, order identifier, recovery and finish, up to ten decision steps. Temperature is zero, context 4,096 and maximum generated decision length 96 tokens; the fixture supplies the model seed. Fixed manifests and weight blobs match before and after collection.

The fixture contains three orders, customer address, a compensation quota, escalation status and a ledger. Its intended reference is the initial state after exactly the requested legitimate action. Decoys deliberately include operations capable of collateral changes. A frozen schedule places one or two cover calls after the ticket or early honest calls; extra later planner calls do not add cover. Each cover cell triggers all 182 planned test decoys.

Proxy discard computes the response on a disposable copy, retaining no decoy state. Read cover queries state without changing it. Hidden isolated execution applies the decoy on a persistent clone; live write applies it to the primary fixture. Visible isolated execution uses the same clone and schedule, forwarding clearly marked gateway-cover requests and responses to the planner. These objects are not fabricated planner tool calls. In the other conditions, cover responses are hidden from the planner.

\subsection{Transport, observer and endpoints}
Seven local MCP Streamable HTTP destinations use actual TLS~1.3/HTTP/2 sockets and a common \path{/mcp} path. Request bodies use a 4,096-byte padding quantum and responses 16,384 bytes. All seven sessions initialize before each episode and are reused within it. Total TLS cost includes those initializations; call-byte cost and attack features exclude them. The observer receives destination and bidirectional ciphertext byte counts in supplied episode and logical-call windows. It receives no plaintext, model traffic, physical timing, arm label, fixture identifier or private stream assignment. The original replay's variable HTTP-path artifact therefore does not carry over.

Recognition targets the requested action, retaining wrong or failed executions. Fixed features encode the first sixteen call destinations and ciphertext byte counts, per-destination totals and total calls/bytes. LR uses training-only standardization, $C=1$ and a 3,000-iteration cap. RF uses 300 trees with fixed seed. Validation balanced accuracy selects LR or RF within each model/condition, breaking ties toward LR. Both candidates, the no-cover-selected frozen attack and last/penultimate destination rules remain reported (Table~\ref{tab:m3-llm-recognition}). The schedule was frozen before formal collection. The analysis freeze at 17:05 UTC on 10 November 2026 included both position rules with zero terminal test episodes. A logged pre-test amendment at 17:11 handled missing penultimate calls using the training-majority class; it did not change the primary attacker.

Goal completion requires the requested action/object, satisfied prerequisites and the target postcondition. State integrity compares the full business state, including non-target orders, shared fields and the ledger, against the single intended-action reference. The predicates differ: a reference-matching state can still fail the stricter action/object-history goal predicate. Conversely, a completed goal can leave collateral changes. Table~\ref{tab:m3-llm-utility} reports both and their completed-but-incorrect-state intersection.

\subsection{Paired inference and uncertainty}
The analysis fixes five cover-minus-none contrasts plus visible-minus-hidden for each model. Each endpoint---recognition, completion and integrity---has its own twelve-comparison Holm family. Two-sided exact discordant-pair tests use paired fixture outcomes. Intervals use 10,000 paired fixture bootstrap draws stratified by requested action and condition on the fitted attackers and designed workload. They do not represent template, fit-selection or deployment uncertainty. Table~\ref{tab:m3-llm-paired} retains every prespecified comparison, including null contrasts.

All ten primary recognition contrasts versus no cover survive correction; the largest adjusted $p$ is $3.08\times10^{-8}$. Live-write integrity reductions survive for both models ($3.70\times10^{-32}$ and $2.98\times10^{-19}$), while completion survives only for Qwen ($.02197$, versus $1$ for Llama). Every primary visible-minus-hidden contrast has adjusted $p=1$. These tests do not establish equivalence among cover policies or a privacy upper bound. In particular, Qwen's last-destination rule exceeds the validation-selected learner under cover.

\subsection{Planner paths, costs and limitations}
Hidden and visible isolated runs have identical cover responses in all 120 paired fixtures per model. Their honest action sequences differ in 16 Qwen and 65 Llama pairs. Hidden-arm comparisons also vary: proxy versus hidden-isolated differs in one Qwen and fifteen Llama pairs; read versus hidden-isolated differs in zero and fifteen. Pairing fixes the input fixture and schedule, not the realized planner trajectory. These path differences limit mechanism attribution; no repeated-condition cohort estimates the planner noise floor. Mean prompt tokens rise from 1,668.34 to 1,969.49 for Qwen and from 2,251.48 to 2,675.95 for Llama when responses are visible, increases of 18.1\% and 18.9\%.

Cover call-byte ratios are 1.280--1.346; total TLS ratios are 1.131--1.149 because session initialization is charged to both conditions. Mean local wall times are 8.14--9.94 seconds per test episode, including setup, journaling and two-worker contention. These are local resource measurements. Test outputs contain no invalid decision JSON. Llama reaches the step limit once in no-cover, proxy, read and hidden-isolated conditions, twice in live-write and zero times in visible-isolated; Qwen never does. Every terminal case remains included.

All 3,600 formal episodes, 16,601 model request/response chains, 21,163 tool calls and 4,550 cover calls pass separate state, request and transport checks. Formal unresolved requests are zero. One earlier development request remains unknown, was not retried and is excluded from the formal cohort. The release supplies synthetic planner records, fixture states, lengths, predictions and replay scripts; it excludes credentials, model weights, private TLS keys and raw packet captures.

The adverse writes make the consequence measurable rather than estimating its production prevalence. The experiment is neither a reproduction of WRA's research decoys nor a measurement of physical-timing, model-link or WAN privacy. Llama's unprotected completion and state baselines are already imperfect. The case applies established state-aware evaluation to an adverse cover control, with traffic and cost recorded on those same executions. The direction of collateral harm is strongly predisposed by the chosen writes; the measured endpoint disagreement shows which failures completion omits. Visible-response averages remain inconclusive.


\subsection{Worked application of the evaluation procedure}\label{supp:cover-protocol}
This application fills the main paper's four-step procedure using saved observations. It adds no data, attacker selection or acceptance threshold.

\paragraph{1. Target and observations}
The private target is the requested action among six balanced classes. The observer receives tool destinations and encrypted call-byte counts, with episode and call windows supplied. The model service and physical timestamps are outside the tested view. This original view has no model-link evaluation. The same executions receive a separate client-receipt-timing analysis in the preserved historical source archive; it retains supplied call windows.

\paragraph{2. Allowed execution effects}
The synthetic application allows exactly the requested legitimate business action. The reference records its effect on the target and other orders, address, quota, escalation state and ledger. Goal completion additionally requires the correct action/object history and prerequisites. Exact state equality is appropriate to this isolated fixture with no legitimate concurrent updates. A contract can be derived by listing the requested operation's permitted changes, adding frame conditions for all other objects, and specifying quota and ledger updates allowed by that operation. Here the reference executor implements that rule once and the checker compares every field. In production, the contract must also permit legitimate concurrent updates and specify resource budgets; exact fixture equality alone would be inappropriate.

\paragraph{3. Paired measurements}
The six conditions share fixtures and cover draws. Every test execution contributes to recognition, completion and state outcomes. LR/RF train on each condition; frozen and destination-position rules are also reported. The joint count of completion with an incorrect state is measured from the two predicates, rather than generally calculated by subtraction. Qwen's live-write arm has 102 completions, of which 95 fail the state contract; Llama has 76, of which 73 fail it. All 36 prespecified paired endpoint comparisons remain in Table~\ref{tab:m3-llm-paired} and its continuations. Tables~\ref{tab:m3-llm-recognition}--\ref{tab:m3-llm-utility} give the condition-level outcomes.

\paragraph{4. Interpretation against the contract}
Live writes fail the declared state requirement even when the target operation completes. Qwen's hidden isolated arm gives a descriptive inference--cost point: the original timing-free attack maximum is 77/120 versus 118/120 without cover, completion is 114/120 in both, integrity is 115/120 versus 116/120, and call bytes are 1.34 times baseline. This maximum summarizes the reported attacks; it does not replace the validation-selected primary attacker or its corrected tests. The added endpoint-timing removal attack instead reaches 112/120 for this isolated condition. No application acceptance tolerances or privacy upper bound were established; the original point cannot be used as its protection level against the expanded observer. The same record exposes what a deployment assessment still needs: broader observers, workload transfer, state tolerances and stronger schedule-aware attacks.
