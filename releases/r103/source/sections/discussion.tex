\subsection{Source dependencies of the two findings}\label{sec:sensitivity}
The transfer matrix separates established conditions from evidence for their Agent application. Model- and tool-side task observations draw on several studies; the decoy/utility mismatch comes from WRA's published text. Live OHTTP refitting and each composition, topology or scaffold transfer axis depend on individual preprints. The two located adapted privacy settings share one source. These dependencies matter more than treating every setting as an independent confirmation.

Source withdrawal tracks thirty-three statements across seven comparisons. The source appendix gives the complete summary and twenty removal scenarios, including individual sources, shared-author groups and publication filtering.

The strict published-text subset retains WRA and CHeaT: query recovery, the decoy/visibility distinction and a defined prevention outcome survive. Other label, population and policy contrasts depend on the wider corpus. Removing one transfer source removes evidence for that axis, rather than changing the other axes. The two local studies add separate controlled evidence. The feedback pilot remains inconclusive after correction. The executed-cover study supports a narrower positive finding: under its adverse-write policy, successful tasks can leave damaged business state. Neither changes the number of literature sources or replicates WRA's decoy mechanism.

\section{Validity, Ethics and Availability}\label{sec:validity}
\paragraph{Evidence scope}
Source locators and withdrawal analysis expose dependencies; index failures and query misses limit coverage. Extraction is author-performed with AI assistance. The two AI recodings are non-blind and dependent; their agreement is a coding-manual-pilot diagnostic, not independent human reliability. The source-specific measurements and our controlled tasks represent different populations.

The forwarding pilot covers six synthetic actions with deliberately category-bearing supplementary records on one host. All its adjusted primary tests exceed .05. The forwarding intervention changes content, encoded length and execution order together; equal tool-call counts do not isolate the first two. Complete-execution and overlap-threshold contrasts are reported alongside the focal phase results. Session resampling, including LR refitting, measures variation within this workload and selected learner family. Recognition receives phase or task windows; localization receives session boundaries and synthetic live background traffic. These conditions leave generalization to other workloads, enterprise background and gateways open. The remote retest changes the backend and network together and uses an alias without pinned weights.

The executed-cover study separately uses 120 unique paired test fixtures, reused phrasings, local destinations and supplied call windows. Its intervals condition on fitted attacks and this constructed workload. Live writes deliberately modify business state; their measured frequency and severity do not estimate production cover harms. The isolated visible-response endpoint differences remain nonsignificant. The weaker Llama baseline and all terminal failures remain in the denominators.

Earlier serial, common-prefix and fixed-composition studies are archived as separate populations in the review artifact, with their failures, inconclusive results and unknown responses. Their outcomes are not pooled with the present experiment.

\paragraph{Ethics and assistance}
Inputs are synthetic, business actions update isolated fixtures, and capture filters restrict collection to the experiment. Remote model use has the account holder's authorization; provider-specific research permission and applicable terms remain unverified. Known failures and unknown responses are retained separately. No human-participant experiment, customer operation or hosted-provider vulnerability finding enters the study. AI tools assisted source extraction, implementation and writing; saved observations and executable analyses support the reported measurements.

Traffic inference can enable unwanted profiling as well as monitoring. The review package contains source-extraction records, synthetic metadata, features, predictions and offline analyses. The executed-cover artifact additionally includes synthetic fixture and planner records sufficient to replay state outcomes; these contain no customer data. Credentials, private user transcripts, raw packet captures and third-party full texts are excluded. Reusable analysis methods still carry misuse potential. A protection summary should report adapted attacks, remaining views and original-task outcomes together; a frozen attack's decline alone can overstate the measured protection.

\paragraph{Availability}
The accompanying review package connects the forty-four-setting evidence matrix, transfer relations, eligibility ledger, source locators and dependency calculations to the claims. Synthetic metadata, predictions, outcome markers, uncertainty scripts and manifests provide an offline verification route. Raw-to-metadata reconstruction needs the excluded captures; the mutable remote alias cannot reproduce fixed weights. The supplement presents the current study and source basis; the artifact also preserves the earlier experimental reports and all saved measurements. Compilable LaTeX and both PDFs accompany the package.

\section{Conclusion}
Classical traffic-analysis knowledge carries into Agent security through explicit access, observation and intervention conditions. The transfer matrix identifies two execution boundaries requiring additional checks: feedback crosses separately protected services, and executed cover changes application work. The fifteen-source evidence map ties these boundaries to actual observation rights and measurements. Among four query/workflow privacy settings, adaptation and original-task utility are located in different records; the published executed-decoy result leaves matched task utility open. The LLM-planned cover study shows the consequence of an incomplete utility endpoint: 95 and 73 completed tasks still have incorrect state under live writes. Both integrity losses survive correction, while visible-decoy effects and the separate feedback pilot remain inconclusive. The practical priorities are to evaluate every permitted residual view and to measure privacy, resource use, task completion and collateral state changes on the same protected executions.
