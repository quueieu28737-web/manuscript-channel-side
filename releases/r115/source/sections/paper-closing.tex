\section{Ethics and Availability}\label{sec:validity}
\paragraph{Ethics}
All experimental inputs are synthetic, and capture filters restrict collection to the experiment. The harmful live-write arm runs on isolated fixtures; no customers or production business states are affected. A hosted retest in Supplement S10 used account-holder authorization; its provider's research terms remain unverified, and it supports no claim in this paper. Traffic inference can enable profiling; our attacks target synthetic tickets only. AI tools assisted extraction, implementation and writing; every measurement is backed by saved observations and executable analysis. Account credentials, hosted-retest captures and transcripts, and third-party full texts are excluded from distribution.

\paragraph{Availability}
The following are available to reviewers at \artifacturl: this paper and its supplement with LaTeX sources; the live-study collection and analysis code, workload and fixture generator, historical timing pools, frozen plans and their hashes; the record-only analysis code and frozen plans; aggregate results and test statistics for every reported table; the floor computation script; the forty-four-setting extraction and source locators; and the blind re-coding sheets, agreement script and adjudication record. These allow reviewers to inspect every implementation, recompute every reported test from the released aggregates and reproduce the floor values. Per-episode records, per-call timestamps and predictions, packet captures and TLS keys are retained locally and are not released; source hashes in the release manifests identify them. Consequently, refitting attacks or recomputing per-fixture quantities, such as the exact role test of Section~\ref{sec:shadow}, is not possible from the release.

\section{Conclusion}
Executed cover leaks through the process that dispatches it. Decoys inserted by a controller lack the model wait that precedes honest tool calls, and this difference is exploitable on real TLS records. Sampling historical gaps hides the shortcut but not the signal, and a shadow planner lowers recognition to a level consistent with its plausible-deniability reference at twice the model work, without yet isolating why. A goal check alone misses collateral state damage. Evaluating such cover requires binding inference, task completion, business state and cost to the same executions, and stating which service capabilities a policy assumes.
