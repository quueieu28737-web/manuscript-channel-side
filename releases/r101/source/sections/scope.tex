\section{Scope and Evidence Construction}\label{sec:scope}
The evidence map connects Agent execution to an observed communication signal, a hidden target and the rights needed to observe it.

\subsection{Inclusion and retrieval}
An Agent uses an LLM to select actions, with external feedback able to affect later action or termination. Client, tool, model and inter-agent interfaces are eligible. Transit observers obtain traffic metadata; contacted recipients see their addressed requests; active auditors also choose probes or challenges. Negative results qualify: PrivacyTrace's metadata/order condition remains included despite scoring below its prior reference~\cite{duan2026privacytrace}. Whole semantic trajectories or browser instrumentation alone enter the adjacent layer, while deliberate covert messages and unrelated prompt-injection defenses fall outside this question~\cite{yao2026intentminer,uitraces2026}.

The fixed eligibility window covers first public submissions from 1 January 2024 through 9 November 2026. Twelve topic queries, a native arXiv query and inclusion rules preceded the retrieval pass; title/publication checks and one-step citation follow-ups completed it. The arXiv query returned 180 records. Public-web queries supplied 33 scholarly candidates across 101 displayed URLs; deduplication yielded 209 records. Two citation leads brought screening to 211: fourteen direct, forty adjacent, 155 outside scope and two unresolved identities. The eligible CHeaT (Cloak, Honey, Trap) seed adds the fifteenth direct source (Figure~\ref{fig:selection-main}). The SSH honeypot entered through citation tracing, illustrating the value of combining retrieval routes. Exact queries and dispositions are in Supplement S2.

\subsection{Extraction and source dependencies}
Forty-four setting records bind source version and locator to access, features, learner, target, population, split, intervention, result and cost. Labels, execution outcomes, failure treatment and policy context are recorded alongside them. WRA and CHeaT are checked against official USENIX texts. One HTTP study has a verified ACM workshop identity, while its detailed measurements come from the extended arXiv version~\cite{borysenko2026http,borysenko2026short}. Twelve other works lack independently verified formal publication identities at the cutoff.

Text reading, displayed-number reconstruction, selected-code inspection and data recomputation have separate entries in the extraction sheet. For example, WRA's decoy and visibility conditions are checked in the published text; OHTTP refitting is checked in selected code. Each check supports its matched premise. Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} reports what remains when sources are removed. Extraction is author-performed with AI assistance and without independent human coding.

For the descriptive summary, every setting receives an access class and intervention purpose. Protected-condition adaptation and original-task utility are counted within the four query/workflow privacy settings. Three identity-obfuscation settings and two adversarial-Agent blocking settings have different objectives; thirty-five settings contain no protection comparison. A located item identifies evidence in the retained record. An unlocated item leaves that question open. Supplement S5 lists the assignments and source locators.
