\title{SoK: Reusing Defense Evidence in LLM and Agent Side Channels}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Side-channel defenses for LLM serving and agents are evaluated across caches, traffic traces and state-changing actions. This SoK examines what those evaluations report together and what can be reused at a new deployment. We map 82 study families and extract 33 mitigation records from 20 families, alongside six contextual records. The inspected sources show a reporting asymmetry: an inference outcome is explicitly bound in 16 of 17 applicable families, utility in 4 of 17, and matched adaptive evaluation in 2 of 11. Accepting partial evidence raises utility to 6 of 17 but leaves matched adaptation at 2 of 11. Among eight families with explicit inference and cost evidence, five place them in different records. Agent privacy and attribution studies supply partial usefulness evidence but no explicitly bound task-utility result in the four-family subset. We organize these findings by observed signal, executed intervention and evaluation setting. A record atlas preserves positive examples, including CachePrune's shared quality-and-cost package, while exposing when costs or outcomes belong to another mechanism. Comparison rules, complete query memberships and filled reporting cards translate the synthesis into candidate selection and deployment measurements. These findings describe reporting in the inspected corpus, with applicability and status sensitivities made explicit.
\end{abstract}
