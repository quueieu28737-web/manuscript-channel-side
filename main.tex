\begin{abstract}
We propose a reporting framework for deciding which defense evidence can be reused when an LLM or agent deployment changes. It organizes the decision around the receiving view, affected execution state, and attacker and workload, and records source-linked protection, function and cost in a reporting card.

Two bounded cases show how the card separates evidence objects. In E4's post hoc synthetic replay, for one Qwen~8B four-chunk configuration, a fixed logistic attacker fitted to each condition has balanced accuracy/AUROC 0.744/0.800 on raw traffic and 0.732/0.773 after batching, on matched topic-held-out native outputs. These are point estimates and residual signal remains. A clean-trained attacker tested after batching scores 0.500/0.678, illustrating why a single threshold can obscure score ranking. In these local replays, response application TLS bytes are 0.381 times the raw value, excluding requests and handshakes. Calibration comparisons are exploratory, with Holm correction limited to two imports per target. R44 separately illustrates task-utility risks of a state-changing cover action in an original retail-sandbox convenience sample. Purposefully selected literature examples provide context.

This is a methodological position paper: the card is a proposal grounded in existing evaluation principles. Its usefulness, reliability and effect on analyst decisions remain to be evaluated; the cases are illustrations rather than validation. Source identities and eligibility remain unresolved in parts of the frozen material, and the examples do not establish field-wide prevalence or deployment effects.
\end{abstract}
