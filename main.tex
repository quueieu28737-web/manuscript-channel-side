\begin{abstract}
Deploying an LLM or agent defense requires deciding which protection, function and cost evidence survives a change of context. This SoK organizes that decision around three boundaries: the receiving view, the affected execution state, and the attacker and workload. Source-linked evidence cards record supported observations, incompatible measurement objects and unresolved reuse questions; analyst benefit and coding reliability are not established. An attack-evaluated map of 19 mitigation families and a purposive six-source defense-first challenge set supply case material, not estimates of reporting prevalence. A frozen retrospective source map also admits formal and design evidence without requiring an empirical attack score. Selected cache and hardware examples distinguish configuration associations from paired measurements and conditional guarantees from empirical results. Coverage, source identity and eligibility remain incomplete. A finite synthetic replay study on three pinned local model artifacts illustrates why a clean-trained attack, an adapted attack and a calibration comparison require separate interpretation; its default identical-parameter controls provide no transfer evidence. The calibration comparisons do not establish a robust transfer effect. Fresh retail-sandbox interactions quantify a deliberately state-changing write condition in a nonrandom, mutable-service-alias cohort. These are bounded applications of established evaluation principles, not a validation of the reporting procedure or a general estimate of deployment harm.
\end{abstract}
